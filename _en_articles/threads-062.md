Last time we said friendship isn't a matter of fate, but a psychological mechanism that can be "worked."
Former FBI agent and instructor Jack Schafer, in The Like Switch, proposes the formula:

Friendship = proximity + frequency + duration + intensity

Simply put:
show up often, keep interactions comfortable, be natural together, and make the other person feel "you're paying attention to them."

This formula works because it captures the underlying psychology of "how humans build trust."
But if you apply it to the 14 major stars of Zi Wei Dou Shu, you'll find that
each star responds to a different level of "intensity" and likes a different "way of interacting."

Some like you to show up often (the Blessing Star, the Seeker).
Some hate clinginess (the Star of Depletion, the Chancellor).
Some like you to get straight to the point (the Resolute Star, the Treasurer).
Some want you to understand their feelings (the Moon, the Star of Obscurity).

The same FBI friendship formula has completely different effects on different major stars.

║ The 14 major stars × The Like Switch

2 | The Resolute Star (Qi Sha): understand the path I want to take, and I'll admire you
The Resolute Star fears "boring small talk" and hates clinginess; it respects only people who dare to get to the point and act crisply.
The more decisive you are, the more comfortable it is; the more you understand it, the more it will remember you.
Key to friendship: courage, fearlessness, getting to the point, being action-oriented.

Most effective strategy (FBI version):
• Proximity: show up often, but keep each time short, quick and clean
• Frequency: short exchanges work best (it hates small talk)
• Duration: don't drag things out, and it will make a point of keeping you around
• Intensity:
Breaking the ice with a stranger: "What you said just now was so bold; I really admire it."
Breaking the ice with a weak tie: "You handle things so fast and decisively; I think that's impressive." "I can understand why you stick to your guns."

3 | The Star of Depletion (Po Jun): make me feel you're interesting, and I'll get close to you
The Star of Depletion likes "difference," "adventure" and "novelty"; the more unique you are, the more you attract it.
Don't be too conservative or too formulaic; it will make a point of getting close to whatever sparks its interest.
Key to friendship: distinctiveness, being unconstrained, nerve, variety, willingness to try (but don't meddle too much in its business).
Most effective strategy:
• Proximity: show up in dynamic, active settings (events, competitions, projects)
• Frequency: frequent, small interactions, but don't cling
• Duration: doing things together works better than chatting
• Intensity:
Breaking the ice with a stranger: "Hey, the way you did that is so cool; I rarely see that." "You're a really interesting person."
Breaking the ice with a weak tie: "The way you do things is so creative; you've inspired me." "That move was clever! I admire it."

4 | The Treasurer (Wu Qu): only if you're genuine will I trust you
The Treasurer believes only in "practicality," "reliability" and "evidence of action."
Don't be vague or flashy, and don't talk too much about empty dreams.
The more down to earth you are, the more at ease it feels.
Key to friendship: steadiness, practicality, sincerity.

Most effective strategy:
• Proximity: it's easiest to get close in work settings or practical situations.
• Frequency: short, frequent interactions plus professional topics work best.
• Duration: doing things together warms things up faster than chatting.
• Intensity:
Breaking the ice with a stranger: "The suggestion you just made is really practical."
Breaking the ice with a weak tie: "You really are reliable and keep your word; I can see how serious you are." "I think you're very dependable."

5 | The Keeper of the Seal (Tian Xiang): be appropriate and polite, and I'll feel comfortable
The Keeper of the Seal cares about "decorum," "etiquette" and "boundaries"; people with taste who respect boundaries attract it most.
Key to friendship: politeness, a sense of boundaries, a sense of decorum.
Most effective strategy:
• Proximity: public spaces and professional settings are the best places to approach it.
• Frequency: light, steady, regular = safe.
• Duration: it doesn't mind chatting; getting information through conversation helps it get familiar with you.
• Intensity: Breaking the ice with a stranger: "You handled that very gracefully just now." "You're really good to your friends."
Breaking the ice with a weak tie: "I really admire your social skill (or the way you handled that) just now." "You have great taste."

6 | The Chancellor (Tian Fu): if you have value, I'll gradually accept you
The Chancellor doesn't make friends on feeling, but on "whether this person is worth it."
It likes interactions that are controllable, stable and of good quality, and only gets close to people it feels are worth knowing deeply.
Key to friendship: being manageable (keeping your interactions within its control), having value.

Most effective strategy:
• Proximity: it's easiest to get close in quiet, stable, good-quality places.
• Frequency: frequent but low-burden; small interactions and short lines work best.
• Duration: being able to sit quietly together over a drink (a chance to show your value) = the Chancellor begins to accept you.
• Intensity:
Breaking the ice with a stranger: "Are you from OO school? I guessed from that bag you're carrying."
Breaking the ice with a weak tie: "You've seemed a bit busy lately. I know XXX, so if there's anything I can help with, let me know." "That idea you just shared was brilliant; I'd love to learn from you sometime."

7 | The Sun (Tai Yang): appreciate me, and I'll light you up
The Sun needs "to be seen," "to be affirmed," "a sense of presence."
The more you appreciate it, the more it shines; the colder you are, the dimmer it gets.
Key to friendship: generous praise, appreciation, affirmation.
Most effective strategy:
• Proximity: show up near settings where it can shine.
• Frequency: a little warmth + high frequency.
• Duration: listening to it talk is the fastest way to get close.
• Intensity:
Breaking the ice with a stranger: "What you just did (or said) was so infectious; I was really moved."
Breaking the ice with a weak tie: "You hosted that really well just now; you're so charismatic." Praise = the Sun's love language (recognize its big-heartedness).
