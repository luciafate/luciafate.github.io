Zi Wei Dou Shu uses the stars to draw "a spectrum map of the human emotional system."
Every star carries both reason and feeling; only the proportions and the ways they show up differ.
In other words, every star is really a "value function bundle":
what it cares about, how it feels, how it habitually acts, all different.

And the Value Function Ilya talks about already has an answer in Zi Wei Dou Shu:
"Your combination of stars + palaces + the Four Transformations and Sha stars that time and environment give your stars' traits
= decide what you care about, how you feel, and how you act."

•  The Sun (Tai Yang):
On the surface very emotional: I want to light up everyone, I want to be good to everyone.
But the underlying logic is:
"Under my rules, everyone is well = order is stable = my reason for existing."
So its warmth is actually love that has been calculated through "order and roles."

•  The Chancellor (Tian Fu):
Emotionally, what it cares about is the reassurance of "everything under control."
Rationally, it calculates resources, risks and the cost of positioning
before deciding whether to act, whether to take things on.
It looks controlling, but it's actually solving for the optimum of "long-term stability."

•  The Emperor (Zi Wei):
On the outside, the emotions of self-respect, caring about face and not being looked down on.
But inside it's calculating: "To maintain my current face and status, do I have enough money, resources and image to support it?"
Many seemingly irrational insistences are actually risk management of the asset of "personal dignity."

• The Strategist (Tian Ji):
Looks rational, logical and orderly, as if it "has no emotions."
But its emotion is:
"As long as things make sense and the logic holds, I feel at ease."
So it handles emotion through "analysis," and trades "reason" for a sense of security.

•  The Guardian (Tian Liang):
Its emotional side is gentle, protecting others, being everyone's support.
Its rational side is evaluating: "Is helping this way better for them in the long run? Does it add value for the organization, for the bigger picture?"
It doesn't dispense "warmth" at random; it's mature goodwill calculated through "experience + consequences."

•  The Blessing Star (Tian Tong):
On the surface very emotional: optimistic, easygoing, wanting the easy road.
But it's actually very good at calculating: "What way gives the lowest cost and the highest comfort?"
So it looks laid-back, but it's actually optimizing life through "saving effort + keeping flexibility."

•  The Keeper of the Seal (Tian Xiang):
Looks like it's all about goodwill and harmony, playing the friendly neighborhood chief.
But what it's best at is doing the arithmetic within a web of relationships:
"Is keeping this relationship worth it in the future? If I say this now, will it hurt anyone?"
Its warmth is a choice made after a precise "relationship abacus."

•  The Moon (Tai Yin):
What shows is gentleness, sensitivity and caring for others,
but what's truly impressive is its resource-allocation brain:
"How do I allocate time, money and emotion to make this home more stable?"
Its empathy isn't simply being moved, but "a long-term budget with caring as its goal."

•  The Seeker (Tan Lang):
Emotionally it's one sentence: "I want to try everything, play with everything."

Rationally, it's building a database through lots of experiences:
"What's fun? What works? What's profitable?" It lives life as an experience-based RPG.
So it looks playful, but it's actually running A/B tests with its life, building up a very clever model of the world.

•  The Star of Depletion (Po Jun):
On the surface impulsive, willing to tear things down and start over, "if I'm unhappy, I flip the table."
But the Keeper of the Seal is opposite it, which means it actually knows "this situation has been worked out; not tearing it down would be worse."
So its outbursts of willfulness are very often a decision of "better to take the initiative than passively remain trapped with no way out."

•  The Resolute Star (Qi Sha):
Emotionally, it's that grit of "I'll get through this even if I have to grit my teeth."
Rationally, the Chancellor opposite gives it a core belief:
"As long as I get through this, my situation will be much better."
So it isn't suffering blindly, but treating pain as an investment that can be traded for future chips.

•  The Treasurer (Wu Qu):
Its emotional needs are very plain: I want visible results, steady money, a sense of security.
Rationally, it calculates: "Is this worth it? Will it last? Can I bear the risk?"
It's down to earth not because it can't dream, but because it knows clearly that "only once you won't starve do you get to make choices."

•  The Star of Restraint (Lian Zhen):
Emotionally, it's very sensitive to temptation, and very attuned to the boundaries of rules.
Rationally, it's superb at calculating: "What's the most efficient way? Where can I slip through, and where must I not step?"
So on the one hand it restrains itself, and on the other it's the best at spotting loopholes in systems;
to it, gray areas are a resource both usable and dangerous.

• The Star of Obscurity (Ju Men):
On the surface all kinds of unease, mental theater, overthinking.
But that's actually it constantly running "scenario simulations" and "risk calculations":
"If I say this, what will they think? If things go wrong, how will I respond?"
Its suspicion is a highly sensitive detection system, helping it catch places where "things haven't happened yet but could go wrong."

You can treat these stars as maps of how emotions and values are configured.
From here, what really matters isn't blindly suppressing emotion, but "learning to recognize, adjust and make good use of this" human emotional system.

║ In the AI era, reclaim control over your emotional life
Put these three things together:
- Ilya, starting from AI, says: without a value function (an emotional system), there's no real intelligence
- Brooks, from the human side, says: excessive rationality makes us lose connection and meaning
- Zi Wei Dou Shu: takes this whole thing apart into stars, Sha stars, the Four Transformations and the changes of palaces with time and environment, drawing "a map of human emotion and choice"

The shared message of all three:
"Emotion isn't the bug that makes you fail, but the core of what makes you 'human,' rather than 'a cheaper AI tool.'"

So rather than pushing yourself to "be more rational" and "not be emotional," try asking instead:

① What does the combination of stars in my Self, Money and Daily Focus palaces care about? What does my "value system" look like?
② What are the impulses, inner conflicts and unease I currently dislike actually protecting?
③ Can I criticize myself a little less, and understand and empathize with myself a little more?

AI will get smarter and smarter, better and better at imitating humans,
but it won't rewrite its life because of love, because of dignity, because of a tiny sense that "something's off,"
let alone, for the sake of a belief, make the choice "I know I'll get hurt, but I'm doing it anyway."

What we truly need to do isn't to train ourselves into more rational machines, but to learn to read our own emotional system, and then, with this unique "human operating system," make the choices only you can make.

This is your true core competitive edge in the AI era.
