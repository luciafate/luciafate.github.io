When many people see "Hua Lu" in their chart during a reading, their first reaction is:
"Awesome! Am I going to get rich?"

But honestly, this is a beautiful misunderstanding.
The meaning of Hua Lu is often understood far too narrowly.

The underlying logic of Hua Lu isn't actually the same as "money."

Open the classic Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu and you'll find a key line:
"Lu is the spirit of fu de."

This "fu de" isn't moral merit in a narrow sense, nor religious reward for good and punishment for evil.
In classical Chinese, "fu de" broadly refers to the benefits, support and smooth going that one can enjoy in life.
So "fu de" here is closer to the good things you can receive in life, such as
opportunity
wealth
reputation
status
support
resources
depending on which star has become Hua Lu.

The Shuowen Jiezi says: "Lu means blessing."
In even earlier forms of the character, 彔 actually looks like a windlass drawing water from a well.
Only once water is drawn up can the fields be irrigated and the harvest secured.

So from its very origins, the character 祿 (lu) was never just an abstract good omen, but a state of
supply, nourishment, harvest, and being able to benefit.

So we can understand "Hua Lu" precisely as an increase in resources.
Remember it in one line: Hua Lu governs "gain."

When a star becomes Hua Lu, it doesn't suddenly turn into an all-powerful lucky star;
rather, its original function, in this setting of time and place,
more easily receives supply, support and return.
Things are easier to push forward,
and opportunities more easily come close.

To make it instantly clear, remember this simple formula:
a star's essential nature × Hua Lu = an increase in a certain kind of resource

For example:
the Treasurer (Wu Qu, core quality: cai, wealth) with Hua Lu
→ financial resources flow in more easily
(only when this appears in the Palace of Money Management or on the Children–Home axis in a timing cycle is it "really" money)

the Moon (Tai Yin, core quality: fu, abundance) with Hua Lu
→ a sense of security, care and resources accumulate more easily
(the Moon is also a wealth star, so in the Palace of Money Management or on the Children–Home axis in a timing cycle, it suggests income)

the Seeker (Tan Lang, core quality: peach blossom) with Hua Lu
→ interpersonal attraction grows stronger, and goodwill in public relations increases

In fact, the Four Transformations describe how people, in different settings of time and place,
"gain, take control, become visible, and fall out of balance."

The Four Transformations of the stars are very important, because they are precisely
where you and your "setting in time and place" act on each other.
If you don't understand the Four Transformations, many signs in a chart won't make sense.
