║ The star system
Why does the same star look completely different in a different palace?
Remember that Zi Wei Dou Shu combines the palace system and the star system?
Palace = the environment of time and space
Star = a person's "personality expression and pattern of ability" in that setting

As soon as the "person" or the "environment of time and space" changes, the "phenomenon" changes with it.
This is why Zi Wei Dou Shu can read "signs of what will happen as time and space change."

[This is actually the principle of changing your fortune too: change the person, change the timing or change the environment, and you have a chance to change the outcome.]

After How to Read Your Own Zi Wei Dou Shu Chart ④ taught you to understand the 12 palaces,
we now come to the main character itself: the personality, abilities and desires of the "person," that is, the "stars."

People's personalities are complex.
Even the same you deals with your parents, your boss, your partner and your friends in different ways,
but all these "different sides of you" are still you.

Zi Wei Dou Shu reads things with the same logic:
everyone's chart has 12 palaces = 12 life scenes,
and the stars = your "behavior patterns and the way your abilities show" in that setting or scene.

So:
are you domineering with your family? Or willful, holding things in, or quietly giving?
The "stars" in the Palace of Home and Property tell you about your relationship with your family.
What are you like at work or school? What are you like facing your boss, or your teachers?
All of this can be seen from the stars in the Palace of Daily Focus!

｜Before you really start learning the major stars, there are 2 things you must know

Many people who start learning Zi Wei Dou Shu go straight to Googling "What is the Treasurer (Wu Qu) star?" or "the Seeker (Tan Lang) star, unfaithful."
But if that's all you do to learn the stars, you'll always be like the blind men feeling the elephant, seeing only part of it,
and you'll never "understand the chart."
There are two reasons:
① A star must be read together with its palace to be accurate
Example:
the Seeker = peach blossom, popularity, loving to experience life, wanting to try everything.
But if it falls in the Palace of Inner Life and Fortune (mind / soul / values),
it won't become "a "promiscuous" mind";
instead it becomes "spiritual exploration, curiosity, a philosophical bent, an affinity for religion, a love of pursuing growth of body, mind and spirit."
Same star → in a different palace, it's only authentic when interpreted according to the meaning of the palace!
[Strictly speaking, you need to look at the stars in this palace and in the opposite palace; palaces work in pairs, remember!]

② Interpreting a star always has to start from its "core quality"
Core quality = the most central, purest essence of the star
(= if you put the star into a fire and everything else burned away, what's left)
Example:
the Moon (Tai Yin) → its core quality is fu (abundance); it's a wealth star.
The Moon symbolizes the mother, the feminine: gentleness, attention to detail, caring, being connected to home, a sense of security, accumulation → so the Moon is the ruler of the Palace of Home and Property ("ruler of XX" means it suits being placed in the XX palace).
In the Palace of Home and Property (family / residence), it means "a sense of stability" (a home with Mom in it: reassurance & security), "building up assets little by little," "practical saving, good at managing family resources" (aren't moms always careful with money?).
