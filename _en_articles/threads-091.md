Do you also often find yourself held hostage by insecurity, anger and anxiety?
I've recently started learning mindfulness meditation, and found it isn't just psychology or a relaxation technique, but an attitude to life. What's more interesting is that it echoes "Hua Ji" in Zi Wei Dou Shu.

What is mindfulness?
Mindfulness: deliberately placing your attention on the present moment, and noticing your body, thoughts and emotions without judgment.
Its three core elements:
•	Intentional attention: deliberately placing your attention on your breath and bodily sensations.
•	A non-judging attitude: being able to notice "I'm judging" without being carried along by it.
•	Focusing on the present: not dwelling on the past, not anxious about the future, just feeling "now" as it is.

These three core elements are practices that help us avoid being held hostage by emotions and outside information.

Zi Wei Dou Shu ║ Stars with Hua Ji
In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the stars represent a person's traits, tendencies and abilities. When a star takes "Hua Ji," it's like a gap appearing in the heart:
•	it symbolizes emptiness, desire and need
•	it hints that a relationship or situation has a problem
•	when desire can't be satisfied, it brings frustration and unease
Simply put, Hua Ji is a gap in the heart.

For example:

•	The Sun with Hua Ji: the Sun's core quality is gui (esteem); it needs status and a voice. With Hua Ji, it's afraid inside of losing its light, easily cares too much about its reputation, at times domineering and controlling, at times doubting itself.
•	The Moon with Hua Ji: the Moon's essence is fu (abundance); it values security and accumulation. With Hua Ji, it's overly anxious about money and feelings; it wants to take care of others, yet when it gets nothing back, it starts keeping score, calculating, and going down rabbit holes.

Hua Ji reminds us to see the gap in our hearts;
mindfulness meditation helps us tell ourselves there's no need to rush to fill it, but to first notice and accept.
