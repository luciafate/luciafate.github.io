In almost every chart reading, I get asked: "Teacher, is my wealth luck good this year?"
But honestly, many people ask the wrong question from the start.
What you should really ask isn't "Will I get rich this year?"
but "Why do I keep feeling I don't have enough money, even though I have an income?"

The economist Easterlin did research with a very interesting result:
comparing across people, those with higher incomes are usually happier too.
But if you follow the same person over many years, you find that as income rises, happiness doesn't necessarily rise with it.

Why?
Because the happiness money brings is often not an absolute value but a [relative value].
If you earn more than the people around you, you feel you're doing well.
But if everyone gets a raise together and you're still not high in the group, you may stay just as anxious and still feel it isn't enough.

So what many people really want to ask isn't about wealth luck at all.
It's:
when will I stop always feeling I don't have enough money?

The problem is that "feeling you have enough money" is never solved automatically by earning a bit more.
It also involves your personality, your judgment of risk, your spending habits, and how you make choices
(BTW, these can actually be seen too, from the natal chart plus the timing cycles).

And for most people, what they care about most is usually something more practical:
when there are signs of money coming in,
when to watch out for losing money,
and what personality traits or habits make them prone to losing money.

Being able to anticipate early, prepare in advance, maximize the benefits and avoid wrong turns:
that is where Zi Wei Dou Shu has real value.

Not hearing "your wealth luck is good this year" and then starting to invest recklessly, charge ahead and spend wildly.

But I have to be honest: what truly changes a financial situation is usually still two things:
financial literacy: your understanding of money and your judgment;
real ability: your capacity to get the market to pay for your value.

If these two things aren't gradually built up,
then however much you ask about wealth luck, however much you want to change your fortune, the effect will be limited.
[Unless you were born into a "good" family (with a fortune to inherit), most people's financial improvement still depends on themselves.]

As for 2026, if you really want to do something,
I'd actually suggest you start with something very small:

tidying your space.

In 2026, the annual Money–Inner Life axis falls in the Yin and Shen positions, which correspond to the directions:
east-northeast
west-southwest

If those two corners of your home happen to have some space, I suggest clearing them and not piling up clutter.
It isn't that you'll suddenly get rich once you've tidied.

It's that a chaotic environment often affects people's emotions, judgment and actions.
The messier the space, the easier it is to procrastinate, get distracted, and make muddled spending or investment decisions.

With a clear space, it's easier to return to a steady, clear-headed state, which helps your wealth luck improve.
This weekend, spend 30 minutes tidying that corner and give it a try!!

So rather than keep asking:
"Is my wealth luck good this year?"

ask yourself first:
Are you ready to catch opportunities?
Have you built the abilities that make you valuable?
Are you making choices that make you less likely to lose money?

Wealth luck has never been waiting for gifts to fall from the sky.
It's whether you're ready.
