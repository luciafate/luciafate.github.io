A quick quiz:
Zi Wei–Tian Xiang (the Emperor with the Keeper of the Seal) with Po Jun (the Star of Depletion) opposite, versus Zi Wei–Po Jun (the Emperor with the Star of Depletion) with Tian Xiang (the Keeper of the Seal) opposite: can you say how these two differ?
Tian Fu (the Chancellor) can have three combinations of stars opposite it. In the workplace, what roles suit each?
With Qi Sha (the Resolute Star) in the Palace of Money Management, the opposite palace can hold Zi Wei–Tian Fu, Wu Qu–Tian Fu or Lian Zhen–Tian Fu. How do these three people's attitudes to money differ?

If you stumbled over the answers,
it's not that you're not clever enough. It's that you've never really unpacked the underlying logic of the stars.

For example:
"The Emperor cares about face." If you think this just means vanity, you haven't really understood it.

The Emperor's core quality is zun, dignity.
Zun = being in a position, within a setting or relationship, that cannot be treated carelessly.

Grasp this essence and you can work out
why the Emperor needs to be seen, why it holds high standards, and why it hates being brushed off most of all.
That is truly learning it.

That is what Zi Wei Dou Shu: An Algorithm, Not Fortune-Telling is about.
Starting from the origins of the characters, it goes all the way through the Four Transformations of the stars, star combinations, and how to read a star in different palaces.
It doesn't give you more labels; it gives you a framework for truly understanding the stars.
