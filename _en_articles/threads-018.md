Judging a person or a matter usually takes information from many sources, weighed together, before you can draw a conclusion.
Many people wear a social mask, but if you watch what they do and say, and the people they associate with, you can usually still pick up some clues.
And another way of judging the information is reading their chart.

Today I want to share with you:
how signs of taking bribes show up in a chart.

The person whose chart this is has civil servant status and is also a very well-known doctor who once worked at a top university medical center; the Taipei District Court recently issued a first-instance judgment in his case.

First, let's define the term.
Taking bribes means a civil servant, with regard to an act within their official duties (whether the act violates those duties or not), demanding, agreeing to, or accepting a bribe or other improper benefit.
Under Articles 4 and 5 of Taiwan's Anti-Corruption Act, the offense of accepting bribes mainly covers civil servants who, in carrying out their duties, accept benefits to obtain illegal gains for themselves or others, such as money, banquets, sexual services or job placements.
Whether or not their duties are actually violated, as long as there is a quid pro quo, the offense may be established.

So to read bribe-taking, a chart usually needs to meet several conditions:
1.	Authority at work
For example, Hua Quan, Hua Ke (visible Ke, hidden Lu: ming ke an lu), or stars of the power-holding type.
2.	Money, or extra benefits, coming in on the Children–Home or Money–Inner Life axis in the timing cycles
Look mainly at the Children–Home axis, and these benefits will be related to the job and its duties.
3.	Values that don't see taking bribes as wrong
That is, a certain degree of acceptance of gray areas, or even illegal acts.
