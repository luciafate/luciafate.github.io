In the previous post, we talked about something many people overlook, yet which is changing the whole era:

emotion isn't noise that gets in the way of thinking; it's the brain's operating system.

It lets you, when there's no data, no algorithm and no model,
still "sense that something's off," adjust your behavior and make choices.
That is the "intuition" and "feeling" that you can't quite explain, yet which often saves you.

This is precisely the biggest difference between humans and AI:
AI is good at rational calculation, but we have "feeling."
And this ability is an important survival tool carved into our genes by tens of thousands of years of evolution.

This "ability to feel" isn't abstract at all.
It's long been hidden in your daily behavior, your decision patterns, your desires and anxieties,
existing in a way you can't see, but that is always at work.

If the first post answered "why do emotions matter?",
the second post takes on a more fundamental and harder question:
how do we understand our own emotional system?

Besides science, is there any other way to understand it?
Psychological tests? MBTI? The Big Five personality test?

The answer: yes, and far more structured than you'd imagine → Zi Wei Dou Shu.

Many people think Zi Wei Dou Shu is fortune-telling, or metaphysics,
but the more I study it, the more I feel it's really more like

a manual for how personality is set off under "person × time × space."
The only difference is whether you can translate it.

The ancients spent thousands of years observing people, organizing
emotions, desires, decision patterns, behavioral drives and changes in time and space
into an astonishingly consistent framework: stars × palaces.

And the most fascinating thing about the system of stars is that
every star carries both reason and feeling; only the proportion differs.

In other words, reason and feeling aren't two opposite ends, but a spectrum.
Every person and every star has their own position on that spectrum,
and that position is your "value function."

║ 1. The Sha stars = the engines of raw emotion
Huo Xing, Ling Xing, Qing Yang and Tuo Luo correspond to: passionate impulse, calm calculation, firm decisiveness, entanglement and not letting go.

In the value system of [reason is supreme], these usually get labeled "bad":
you should control impulses, suppress emotions, not overthink.

But Zi Wei Dou Shu's perspective is completely different: these raw emotions are exactly what drives human action.

- Without impulse, you wouldn't bravely try things
- Without inner conflict, you wouldn't think things through carefully
- Without passion, you wouldn't give it your all
- Without keeping score, you wouldn't weigh pros and cons

What looks "irrational" on the surface is actually your most primal engine.

║ 2. The Four Transformations = four scripts by which traits and emotions are pushed under stimuli from the environment of time and space

Stars don't stay in one state forever.
As time, space and situations change, the same trait grows different faces. This is the Four Transformations in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Lu, Quan, Ke, Ji.
You can understand them simply like this:

- Hua Lu: gaining from this trait, with extra benefits and extra resources
- Hua Quan: because of this trait, wanting control and taking on more responsibility, even with a sense of doubling
- Hua Ke: this trait is seen, spoken of, comes into being
- Hua Ji: a gap, longing and dissatisfaction arise around this trait, and you want to pursue it

What many people fear most is Hua Ji,
because it carries lack, wanting to make up for it, wanting to pursue, and things easily going wrong.

But seen through Ilya's ideas, "Hua Ji" is actually one of the sources of power for the "value function," the emotional system:

- you feel a "lack," so you want to "fill" it
- you feel "something's off," so you want to "adjust"
- you feel "unsatisfied," so you want to "grow"

"Your imperfection is the source of your action."
Hua Ji isn't a curse, but a reminder: "This is what you truly care about."

║ 3. Sha Po Lang = execution driven by desire

There's a classic combination in Zi Wei Dou Shu: Sha Po Lang (Qi Sha, Po Jun, Tan Lang: the Resolute Star, the Star of Depletion and the Seeker).

This group represents an action system ignited by strong desire: once they decide "I just want it," they'll find every possible way and be determined to make it happen.

In a world where reason is supreme, desire is often a dirty word:
you should restrain desire, delay gratification, stay calm and rational.

But Zi Wei Dou Shu would say:
- without the desire for a better life, you won't take the initiative to change
- without a hunger for growth, you won't take risks and break through
- without the voice of "I just really want it," your drive to execute won't be ignited

AI can be given "goals," but it has no real "wanting."
It carries out instructions, but it won't stay up late, agonize, or be happy over a tiny bit of progress.

"Human desire is, in fact, the most powerful driving force."

║ Summary
What Ilya calls the Value Function is, in Zi Wei Dou Shu:

"Your combination of stars + palaces + the Four Transformations and Sha stars that time and environment give your traits decide what you care about, how you feel, and how you act."

So what kind of "emotional system" does each of the 14 major stars represent?
And which kind is your combination of stars?
