What matters most with the stars isn't memorizing a pile of adjectives, but understanding the essence of their core quality (hua qi).
Grasp the core meaning, and you can extend outward from there.

When beginners learn the Sun (Tai Yang) and the Emperor (Zi Wei), it's easy to mix the two up,
because both give a sense of "having a position, influencing others, being easily seen."
But go back to their core qualities and you'll find the fundamental difference.

The Sun's core quality is gui, esteem.
Gui here means: a position and responsibility that form in public view because one is seen and recognized as having value.

The Sun's gui isn't only about being seen.
It carries a sense of "illuminating," "setting the direction," "taking responsibility in public," and "asking others to align with a standard."

You can think of the Sun as the sun in the sky.
It burns itself to light others, but its light and heat also become a kind of standard.
Others can't just enjoy its light and warmth; they also have to bear the demands, rules and sense of direction it brings.
So the core of the Sun is to "shine" direction, standards and responsibility into view, so everyone can see them and align with them.

The Emperor's core quality is zun, dignity.
Zun here means:
being in a position, within a setting or relationship, that must not be treated lightly.

The Emperor's zun is about "position."
Unlike the Sun, it doesn't keep shining and lighting everyone up.
But when it is present, people know:
this person must not be taken lightly.
This position must not be casually shaken.
This position must not be ignored at will.

So the Sun's gui is about visible public value and responsibility.
The Emperor's zun is about one's position in a setting or relationship, and not being taken lightly.
Put simply:
the Sun shines direction and standards into view.
The Emperor makes a position something that must not be treated lightly.
