This line is something I've come to realize recently.
Besides reading books and charts, while writing my e-book lately a question keeps popping up:
what are people actually pursuing in this life?

I used to think it was things like success, wealth, love, achievement, meaning, recognition.
But more and more I feel these are only the surface.

What people are really dealing with all along comes down to three things:
→ Survival
how to avoid scarcity, danger and losing control

→ Positioning
Where am I in the food chain? What is my worth? Will I be replaced?

→ Connection and continuity
How do I build relationships with the world, and leave something behind?

But the deeper realization is this:
many people aren't failing to try.
They're using an outdated framework of values to interpret today's life.

If you believe being good, obedient and not making trouble is what keeps you safe,
you'll keep suppressing yourself.

If you believe a sense of security can only be given by others,
you'll keep clinging to them.

If you think hard work will always be seen,
you'll keep doubling down on effort instead of checking your direction.

In the context of the Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu,
the core qualities of the 14 major stars essentially correspond to the various abilities people need to live in the world.

1. The Emperor (Zi Wei): the function of positioning
People can't live without a sense of position.
You need to know who you are, what you're worth and where you should stand.
The Complete Book calls the Emperor "the honored one of the central heavens" and "the imperial seat."
So what the Emperor really deals with isn't only nobility and self-respect,
but whether you can hold your own position steady, rather than letting the world define you.

2. The Chancellor (Tian Fu): the function of managing resources
People can't live on ideals alone; they also need to store grain, allocate and keep things stable.
The old text says the Chancellor governs wealth, property and livelihood.
So what the Chancellor really deals with is
whether you can hold on to your resources and keep your life's system running steadily.

3. The Resolute Star (Qi Sha): the function of deciding and cutting losses
People need not only to build, but also to cut off.
When it's time to cut ties, do you dare?
The old texts write the Resolute Star up as very fierce, but they also state plainly that it is "the senior general of the Dipper."
So the Resolute Star isn't simply fierce;
it's whether, under high pressure or in a state of endless dragging on, you have the ability to step in and decide.

4. The Sun (Tai Yang): the function of reaching outward and putting out value
People don't merely stay alive;
they also need to be able to illuminate, express, take responsibility and have an influence.
The old text says the Sun "gives a person an air of nobility, able in both letters and arms."
So what the Sun deals with is
whether you can send your value out so the world can see it.

5. The Moon (Tai Yin): the function of reserves and inner stability
People can't keep charging outward; they also need to be able to gather in, nurture and prepare.
The old text says the Moon "becomes fu (abundance)" and is the ruler of the Palace of Home and Property.
So what the Moon deals with is
whether you can slowly cultivate a sense of security and build up a foundation of reserves for yourself.

6. The Star of Restraint (Lian Zhen): the function of boundaries and norms
Living in a group, people must deal with rules, roles and taboos.
The old text says Lian Zhen "in the Dipper governs ranks; in the chart governs authority and orders."
So what the Star of Restraint deals with is
whether you can hold your boundaries, judge what is appropriate, and keep order from spinning out of control.

7. The Keeper of the Seal (Tian Xiang): the function of coordinating within institutions
People don't only fight alone; they also need to be recognized and given a place within institutions.
The old text says Tian Xiang "is the star that governs ranks; its core quality is called the Seal; it is in the position of assisting the emperor."
So what the Keeper of the Seal deals with is
whether you can find a position within the rules, so that you are legitimately recognized by institutions.

That's 7 stars picked at random to reason through. Does this kind of interpretation help you understand the stars better?~
