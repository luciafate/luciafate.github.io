Today I don't feel like talking about Zi Wei Dou Shu; I'm sharing my Buddhist study notes instead.
Honestly, when I first came to Buddhism,
it was only because that line from the Diamond Sutra, "like a dream, an illusion, a bubble, a shadow, like dew, and like lightning,"
was so beautiful (I'm a shallow person),
and I wanted to know what it actually meant.

I've written a pile of "Buddhist study" notes, but I'm still just an ordinary mortal,
loving food, fun and enjoyment, with no aptitude whatsoever for enlightenment. (Don't forget to invite me when there's eating, drinking and fun to be had.)

First, a point many people misunderstand:
Buddhism isn't a body of "certain knowledge,"
it isn't a standard answer, nor an eternal truth.
It's closer to
a dynamic philosophical system that keeps changing shape, branching and evolving with the times, cultures and regions.
(Because the teachings in different sutras are often not the same, and even contradict each other ← there's an element of evolution in this!!)

║ What problem was Buddhism originally responding to?
In the Buddha's time (around the 5th century BCE),
the mainstream thought in India was Brahmanism:
◎ the soul is immortal
◎ the caste system is the will of heaven
◎ if you suffer now, it's karmic retribution from a past life, and you should accept your fate

This system was very stable, and very well suited to a "hierarchical society,"
but for those at the bottom, there was almost no way out.

The Buddha raised a completely different question:
is what keeps people suffering
really just the system, their birth, their fate?
Or is it that
the way people understand "I" itself creates suffering?

║ Buddhism redefined "oppression":
◎ suffering isn't the "identity you deserve"
◎ suffering isn't the will of heaven
◎ suffering isn't because you're not good enough

Rather, it's a "cognitive pattern" that runs automatically,
starting up again and again in you.

So Buddhism did three very radical things:
◎ It denied "ownership of a soul"
→ there's no fixed you who must bear everything forever

◎ It denied "the legitimacy of suffering"
→ suffering can be understood, and it can be stopped

◎ It denied "that birth determines eligibility for liberation"
→ liberation doesn't depend on bloodline, on gods, or on priests

║ Buddhism really has only one core question:
why do people
keep starting up the same mechanism of suffering?
Buddhism expresses this understanding in four Chinese characters:

[Dependent origination, emptiness of inherent nature]

In plain words:
suffering doesn't have a single cause,
nor is it that someone is harming you;
it's one condition after another,
one misperception after another,
a process that runs automatically because of your cognitive structure.

That's why Buddhism speaks of the "Twelve Links of Dependent Origination." (The chain of twelve links = the automatic process that produces suffering: ignorance → mental formations → consciousness → name and form → the six sense bases → contact → feeling → craving → clinging → becoming → birth → aging and death)

Suffering isn't a result, but an automated program.

║ How do you become free of suffering?
Not by escaping the world,
but by letting "the mechanism that produces suffering" stop starting up.

Three levels of practice:

1\. Precepts (sila): stop the losses, cut off the causes
Stop doing things that turn back on you.
It isn't a moral demand, but seeing clearly how "causes" keep the process of suffering going.
In plain words: stop doing things that keep the pain snowballing.

2\. Concentration (samadhi): aware, without following
When thoughts and emotions arise, stay aware without being pulled along,
so the circuit of automatic reaction is broken right there.
In plain words: emotions and thoughts appear, but you don't react right away.

3\. Wisdom (prajna): insight into non-self
See thoroughly that "I" is only a temporary aggregation of the five aggregates;
there is no permanent subject, and suffering loses the ground it clings to.
In plain words: see clearly that "I" is only a temporary combination; the suffering isn't me.
That "I" you cling to so tightly
was never an entity at all.

And so…only then does the whole system truly shut down.

║ Summing up Buddhist "liberation":

Buddhism doesn't ask you to accept unfairness,
nor to leave the world in pessimism.

It lets you stop being held hostage by that
"cognitive structure that keeps copying suffering."

In this sense,
the caste system is only an outer cycle of rebirth;
the real cycle of rebirth is in the mind.

║ What is rebirth?
As long as "this set of conditions that generates suffering" is still running,
it will inevitably, in some vessel,
appear again as "the phenomenon of rebirth."

Zi Wei Dou Shu and the philosophical core of Buddhism
are both bodies of learning I believe in,
ones that can help people live more awake.

the Buddhist study notes of an ordinary person who loves food, drink and fun.
