║ Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches: the language of time and space
1\. Why do the Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches exist?
To keep track of time and the seasons, people, starting from the needs of daily life and farming, developed ways of counting time based on cycles of "ten" and "twelve."
These were later combined into the "stem-branch system of timekeeping":
the ten Heavenly Stems (Jia, Yi, Bing, Ding, Wu, Ji, Geng, Xin, Ren, Gui)
and the twelve Earthly Branches (Zi, Chou, Yin, Mao, Chen, Si, Wu, Wei, Shen, You, Xu, Hai).
Paired in alternation, the two form the sixty-year cycle of the "sixty jia zi," the oldest coordinates of time in Eastern fortune-telling.

2\. The Heavenly Stems: the moving force of time
In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Heavenly Stems represent "the changes produced as time moves forward."
That is the power of time:
it makes the qualities of the stars (= people's qualities) change in different ways as time goes on.

3\. The Earthly Branches: coordinates of space and rhythm
The Earthly Branches aren't just the 12 zodiac animals; they also stand for the 12 months, the 12 two-hour periods of the day, and directions.
The ancient clock, the sundial: at noon, the sun is highest in the sky, due south;
at that moment the gnomon's shadow is shortest and points due north.
So "the Wu hour" doesn't only mean midday; the direction of the branch Wu also stands for south.
As the sun rises in the east and sets in the west, the direction of the shadow changes with time. The Earthly Branches follow exactly this natural rhythm,
dividing a day into 12 two-hour periods that correspond to 12 directions.
In the chart, every palace has an Earthly Branch, combining the rhythm of time with a direction in space.
This is also why a Zi Wei Dou Shu chart is really a projection of "time × space."
