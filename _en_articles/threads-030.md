Relationship palaces (the six kinship palaces): you're not putting on airs; you're asking for a "sense of position."
When the Emperor (Zi Wei) in your chart falls in the Palace of Love and Partnership, the Palace of Parents and Elders, the Palace of Siblings, the Palace of Children and Creation, or the Palace of Home and Property, you become especially sensitive to "appropriate limits" in how you interact in that relationship.

You don't necessarily have to be the boss, and you don't have to control the other person.
What you really care about is whether, in this relationship,
there are boundaries, there is order, and you are taken seriously.

Because relationships are never flat; they have rank.
When the Emperor falls in the Palace of Parents and Elders, the Palace of Siblings or the Palace of Home and Property, you are especially likely to feel an unspoken "friction over position" within your family of origin.

As a child, you had no resources, and couldn't really have fought for power.
What you truly wanted was only one thing:
"In this relationship, am I treated as someone who matters?"
I don't want your place; it's just that you can't treat me as if I'm not there.

Stage one: defense when young
If this "sense of position" goes unanswered for a long time, the Emperor in a relationship palace sets off a psychological defense.
Your first reaction isn't to work hard to prove yourself, but to raise your own position in your mind first. You may become picky and haughty, especially sensitive to what elders say and do, trying to use a lofty stance to force them to recognize your worth.

As you grow older, once you can support yourself and have the confidence of independence, if the way you interact still hasn't changed, your defensive pattern in this relationship switches.

You no longer react childishly or make a scene; instead you use "financial independence" to buy equal footing in the relationship.
You still give your parents an allowance, still come home for New Year's Eve dinner, and none of the outward obligations are missing. But psychologically, you've already shown them out of your inner territory.
This is a very calm kind of defense: using courtesy, distance and detachment to protect a self-respect that must not be trampled again.

From a psychological perspective, this is exactly the typical reaction of people with high self-esteem when their dignity is wounded in a close relationship. When the other person is family or a partner you can't simply cut off, the brain, to avoid being hurt again and again, starts "emotional detachment": I withdraw my feelings, so your disregard can no longer hurt me.
That kind of "perfect politeness, treating each other like guests" isn't weakness; it's a very sophisticated psychological defense.

When I'm courteous to you, I'm actually sending a signal: I've done my duty, and you can no longer shake my position.

Stage two: evolving after midlife, when the relationship enters the years of the Sun
But the script of this palace doesn't stay stuck in cold war and distance forever.

When life reaches the fourth ten-year cycle (around the age of forty), whether the ten-year cycles run forward or backward, the movement of the timing charts eventually pushes this lesson into the field of the Sun (Tai Yang).

This is a crucial turning point. By now you've built your own territory in the real world: perhaps a career, your finances, or a small family of your own.

You're no longer the child looking up to parents and elders, struggling for their approval.

Once you truly have a position of your own, the Sun's qualities of taking on, caring for and shining light on others are activated. As you watch elders who are growing old, whose rank has in fact weakened, the knot that has been stuck in your heart for so long often loosens at this stage.

You evolve from a hedgehog-like defense into a lofty kind of tolerance. This isn't giving in, but true equal footing: "I still take responsibility for caring for you, but even if I never get your approval, I know very well what I'm worth."
