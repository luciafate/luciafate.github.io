║ Zi Wei Dou Shu
Each of the fourteen major stars has a different tendency in recovery. Which kind are you?

The Chancellor (Tian Fu) | Restoring a sense of control
Pain point: the need for control goes off the charts, wanting to control everything
First aid: don't lock the door of your heart, and don't control everything down to the last detail. Not being held hostage by heartbreak is what truly letting go means
Long term: reorganize your finances and routine to restore a steady rhythm; you can focus on work to shift your attention

The Moon (Tai Yin) | Restoring inner security
Pain point: don't ruminate alone late into the night until dawn, wearing yourself down, going down rabbit holes and blaming yourself
First aid: four things for sleep hygiene (a fixed wake-up time, screens off 1 hour before bed, washing with warm water, a one-page emotion journal)
Long term: one deep, positive conversation a week; avoid keeping score and wanting revenge

The Seeker (Tan Lang) | Keeping going with new experiences
Pain point: the sensory void wants to be filled with parties and fast-food relationships
First aid: don't fill the emptiness with alcohol, sex or flirtations. Make a list of "sensory substitutes" (cooking, scents, exercise)
Long term: avoid revenge socializing and getting addicted to flirtation. Find other interesting things, a short trip / a new class / dance or art, and let physical activity carry you out

The Star of Obscurity (Ju Men) | Clarifying the unease inside
Pain point: easily falls into suspicion and arguing with itself; the more it thinks, the messier it gets
First aid: write three columns, emotions, unease and facts, and tell yourself: "It's not that I'm not good enough; it's that they don't know how to appreciate me"
Long term: practice dialectical writing (I feel... because... I need...); seek professional help when necessary, so your thoughts have an outlet again

Some wounds can't be rushed. A little each day, find your way back to your own rhythm.
