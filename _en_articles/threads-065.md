If the "Palace of Self, Palace of the Outer World or Palace of Daily Focus" in your natal chart or timing charts
holds a combination from the Emperor (Zi Wei) star family,
then at work, at school, and when facing teachers or superiors, you'll show a common trait:
you want to be the emperor too.

You want to be respected; you can't lose face, can't be ignored, can't go unseen.
Any scene involving "status, evaluation, a stage" will stir your Emperor instinct:
I want to stand on top, not underneath.

When a combination from the Emperor star family appears:
the natal Palace of Daily Focus = your inborn attitude and abilities in the focus of your daily life.
For example, with the Emperor and the Seeker (Zi Wei–Tan Lang) in the same palace, the personality you were born with makes you, in daily life, someone who cares about face, is fun and loves to play, but also needs a sense of presence.

The Palace of Daily Focus in the timing charts = the people, events and things at school or work you'll meet during this period, the type of superior, the sources of pressure, and your working ability.
If the timing-chart Palace of Daily Focus is Zi Wei–Tian Fu, you'll want attention, resources and power all the more,
and you'll be especially prone to "power struggles" or clashes of opinion with your superiors.

The palaces also symbolize "how people treat each other."
For example: if the Palace of Friends and Associates holds Zi Wei–Tian Xiang, with Po Jun opposite,
you'll be drawn to capable friends with status and good social skills (you'll want to make friends of this type).
You bring passion (Po Jun) and tact (Tian Xiang) to your friends,
but you also want to be the one in the relationship who is "seen and respected" (because of the Emperor star).

So,
when the Emperor falls in your Palace of Self, Palace of the Outer World or Palace of Daily Focus,

the bosses, teachers, superiors and colleagues you meet during this period
will also be more likely to bring competition and tug-of-war over "face / power / status."

1\. Why does the Emperor star clash over face so easily at work?
Because Zi Wei Dou Shu isn't mysticism; it's human nature:
the traits of the Emperor star:
■ cares about position
■ cares about evaluation
■ cares about how others see it
■ cares about whether it's the one in charge
The Emperor star = the emperor star.
When it meets any position "higher than itself," its instinctive reaction is easily: I'm an emperor too.
So:
the Emperor in the Palace of Self → born with an imperial air
the Emperor in the Palace of Daily Focus → at work, needs a stage, control and status
the Emperor in the Palace of the Outer World → must shine when out in the world, can't lose
the Emperor in the Palace of Friends and Associates → friends had better have status, and it wants to be respected by its friends too
the Emperor in the Palace of Love and Partnership → can't lose face in love; the partner should submit to me
the Emperor in the Palace of Children and Creation → hopes the children become dragons and phoenixes, and compares them too
the Emperor in the Palace of Home and Property → criticizing the family = stepping straight on a landmine

So you see: in different palaces, the Emperor shows up completely differently.
But underneath it's the same: face, dignity, a sense of position.

2\. Your boss is an Emperor star: how do you get along?
The Emperor-type boss has four major traits:
loves face, loves being flattered, loves control, easily hurt
Zi Wei Dou Shu is actually psychology too (how people treat each other):
protecting self-esteem
the need for face
the desire to dominate
So you mustn't:
✘ contradict them in public
✘ leave them no way to back down gracefully
✘ make them feel ignored
✘ make them feel you don't respect them
✘ directly challenge their status

The way to survive at work with an Emperor-star boss with "emperor syndrome":
✔ give them a stage
✔ give them the illusion of control
✔ give them respect
✔ say it in private, not in public
✔ turn objections into "asking for the boss's guidance"
✔ always let them choose first, rather than letting them feel forced to accept
This is the basic skill for "surviving safely" at work.

3\. Six kinds of Emperor bosses: the most complete guide to avoiding landmines
Here are six kinds of Emperor bosses, corresponding directly to the real workplace situations you'll meet:

1\. Zi Wei–Tian Fu (Yin and Shen positions) with Qi Sha opposite = the most concerned with face × the most concerned with gain × the most insistent type
Key trait: "What I say is everything."

Your way to survive at work:
✔ report right away (a sense of honor)
✔ give them "option A/B" to choose from (you approve of both A and B; it just gives them the illusion of control)
✔ use data so they feel they made the decision themselves (a rational way to back down)

2\. Zi Wei–Tian Xiang (Chen and Xu) with Po Jun opposite = nobility × order × goes all-out when it snaps
Traits: mild on the surface, rebellious inside. Seems rational, may suddenly flip the table.

How to survive:
✔ the material you give must be correct, by their standards (Tian Xiang has order / aesthetics)
✔ give the system first, while also preparing room for flexibility (understand their two sides)
✔ talk about feelings in private (both stars care about face; never provoke them in public)

3\. Zi Wei–Qi Sha (Si and Hai) with Tian Fu opposite = the most efficient × the fiercest but most capable type
Traits: you find them harsh, but they're actually criticizing the issue, not you.

How to survive:
✔ reports must be quantifiable (results-oriented)
✔ fast and correct (they worship efficiency)
✔ don't be thin-skinned (they're scolding the matter, not you)

4\. Zi Wei–Po Jun (Chou and Wei) with Tian Xiang opposite = the dreamer who talks vision × the short-lived enthusiasm type
Traits: the boss's eyes light up when talking about vision and dreams; seems rule-abiding and in line with the system, but in execution may keep changing direction.

How to survive:
✔ translate the boss's vision into three doable steps
✔ you control the details; leave the stage to the boss
✔ you'll often have to finish the follow-up they didn't do (clean up after them)

5\. Zi Wei–Tan Lang in the same palace (Mao and You) = popular × turns work into a game × needs the right atmosphere
They're probably the easiest boss to get along with in the Emperor family!
How to survive:
✔ make your reports tell a story
✔ only pitch when they're in a good mood
✔ hold them with time frames (you need to manage upward, or things will drag)

6\. Zi Wei and Tan Lang facing each other (Zi and Wu) = easy to get along with on the outside × an emperor inside × loves being admired
How to survive:
✔ report at a fixed rhythm (a sense of security)
✔ give them a way to back down (never make them lose face to their face)
✔ make them feel "they taught you something" (a ruler's sense of superiority)

4\. The common code for all Emperor bosses
Whatever the type, as long as you do the following:
✔ a sense of formality
✔ face
✔ respect
✔ the illusion of decision-making power
✔ never contradicting them publicly
✔ you hold things up behind the scenes, and leave the stage to the boss

Do these six things,
and there's no Emperor boss you can't handle.
