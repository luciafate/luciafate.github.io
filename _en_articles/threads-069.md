Let me tell you a story.
If someone told you, "Comets are actually alien spaceships," what would your first reaction be? You'd most likely think this person had lost it.
What if I added a few more credentials:
✅ the longest-serving chair in the history of Harvard University's astronomy department (9 years);
✅ more than 800 published scientific papers, and director of the Institute for Theory and Computation at the Harvard–Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics;
✅ a member of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology at the White House, and chair of the Board on Physics and Astronomy of the US National Academies;
✅ named by Time magazine in 2012 as one of "the 25 most influential people in space"

Then it isn't as simple as "crazy."

This professor is Avi Loeb.

In 2017, he claimed that the interstellar object humans had observed, 'Oumuamua, was actually a light-sail spacecraft made by an alien civilization.

In 2025, he said again: "The comet 3I/ATLAS may be a hostile alien spacecraft."

For eight whole years, every time an interstellar object appeared, he stepped forward to say: "That may be the creation of an alien civilization."
Each time he was torn to shreds by the scientific community, and each time, it was proven to be a natural object.

If the story stopped here, he'd be nothing more than a crazy professor seen as an "eccentric old crank."

What's interesting is that, at the same time as he was saying in public that "this is an alien spaceship,"
he was writing detailed analysis and reasoning on his own blog, often concluding: "The most likely explanation is actually a natural object."

Is he a scientist, or a madman who believes "the whole world is drunk and I alone am sober"?

1\. A heresy that began with a "messenger"
On October 19, 2017, an object burst into the solar system and was named 'Oumuamua, Hawaiian for "messenger."
It was long and thin like a cigar (about 400 meters long, with a length-to-width ratio rare in the solar system), and as it left the Sun, it didn't slow down but sped up. Astronomers all over the world were watching it, yet no one could fully explain it.

A year later, Loeb published a paper in the top academic journal The Astrophysical Journal Letters:
"This may be a spacecraft from an alien civilization."
The scientific community exploded.
Some criticized him for "pandering to the public," others called it "utterly absurd."
His academic career seemed to hit rock bottom.

2\. Scholar? Or marketing genius?
After hitting rock bottom, he did something no one expected: he wrote a book.
In 2021, Extraterrestrial: The First Sign of Intelligent Life Beyond Earth was published and immediately caused a sensation.

More than ten producers came knocking, his schedule filled with show invitations, and he even joked: "If it's made into a movie, I hope Brad Pitt plays me."

And then, the money came too.
Several wealthy people offered to fund him with US$1.75 million, more than ten times NASA's funding for similar UFO research.
He founded the "Galileo Project," using AI telescopes to search for alien civilizations.

In 2023, he led a team to the Pacific seabed and spent US$1.6 million retrieving 50 metallic spherical fragments, claiming they might be remnants of "alien technology."
The scientific community was in an uproar once again, but he responded with a smile and kept experimenting.

3\. What exactly is he doing? A madman of both reason and feeling
Loeb's behavior looks very much like a "double performance."
On his academic blog, he admits that the inference of a natural object is more likely correct.
In his public narrative, he boldly throws out the hypothesis of an "alien spaceship."

You could say he's pandering to the media and chasing fame and fortune;
you could also say he's pushing science forward in a different way.
Because under the standard academic process and review criteria, highly controversial research like this finds it very hard to get resources,
and by using a controversial narrative, he dragged the unpopular topic of UFOs back into the spotlight, and cleverly got funding.

He challenges the serious boundaries of traditional academia,
redefines "influence" through the power of narrative,
and between reason and imagination, between scholarship and public relations,
he maintains a fascinating tension.

4\. Avi Loeb and Zi Wei Dou Shu: the Strategist (Tian Ji) × the Star of Depletion (Po Jun)
Seeing Avi Loeb's story, let me help you understand the Strategist star more deeply!
The Strategist's core quality is shan (seeking better ways): good-hearted, changeable, good at thinking, good at observing.
Rational and strongly logical, quick-thinking, able to catch changes instantly and recalculate its reasoning.

Outsiders think he changes his mind several times a day, but in fact he has already worked through every possibility in his head, and revised (or arrived at) his conclusion.

And the Strategist is always in hidden combination with the Star of Depletion. The Star of Depletion represents creativity and subversion; its core quality is hao (depletion): ignoring what the world thinks, burning itself out for a dream. Romantic, crazy, fatal.

The Strategist's reason, paired with the Star of Depletion's fervor, is the prototype of Avi Loeb:
writing papers with one hand, stirring up storms with the other; understanding both the rigor of science and the allure of a story.
He is both a scholar and a performer,
using one controversy after another to force humanity to look up at the stars again from a different angle.

5\. Becoming "a complex person"
Avi Loeb's story reminds us:
sometimes "right" and "wrong" aren't what matters most;
what matters is whether you can find your own rhythm between reason and imagination.

Just as the Strategist star teaches us:
in a world that changes in an instant, doubt and belief can coexist.

True cleverness isn't choosing only one side to stand on,
but being able to see a bigger truth within contradiction.
