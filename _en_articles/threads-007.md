Beginners often mix these up, but the mechanisms are completely different.
First, let's pin down the essence of the two stars.

Lian Zhen, the Star of Restraint: its core quality is qiu, literally "confinement."
Qiu carries the sense of boundaries, proportion, frameworks, norms and the limits of responsibility.

When Lian Zhen deals with something, a set of judgments runs automatically in its head:
Is this acceptable?
Where does responsibility end?
Does this cross the line?
Is this within acceptable limits?
Whether in work, love, partnerships or processes, Lian Zhen first wants to draw that line.

Tan Lang, the Seeker: its core quality is peach blossom.
Peach blossom isn't only romantic luck; it also includes
desire, attraction, drawing close, wanting more, the flow of resources, exchanges of goodwill, and relationships opening up.
The Seeker's energy makes people want to come closer, to exchange, to work together, and sets relationships and needs in motion.

So when Lian Zhen meets Tan Lang, there is only one core question:
how do you strike the balance between boundaries and desire?

But "facing each other" and "sharing a palace" work in completely different ways.

Lian Zhen and Tan Lang facing each other: suppose Lian Zhen sits in your Palace of Self and Tan Lang in the opposite palace.
You have Lian Zhen's sense of boundaries, but the way you show those boundaries carries the Seeker's methods:
goodwill, charm, communication, pooling resources, terms of exchange, cultivating relationships.

For example: you want a partner to submit documents to your specifications.
You won't simply tell them, "These are the rules; please comply."
You might first build a relationship, find out what they need, show them how this benefits them too, and finally get them willing to meet your standard.

This is Lian Zhen and Tan Lang facing each other.
The boundary is Lian Zhen's; the method is the Seeker's.
You have your standards, but you use a warmer, more exchange-savvy, more approachable way to bring the other person inside that line.

Lian Zhen and Tan Lang sharing a palace is completely different.
Lian Zhen's judgment of boundaries is still there. It isn't without proportion or standards, but that judgment itself is blended with the Seeker's elements.

That means:
your line is no longer entirely cool, detached and purely rational.
It may be influenced by
I like this person
This opportunity is too tempting
I want this resource
I can't bring myself to turn down this favor
This partnership looks promising
This relationship makes me want to get a little closer
and so on,
so the line you started with becomes flexible, and may even need to be reassessed.

So the common state of people with Lian Zhen and Tan Lang sharing a palace isn't "having no boundaries." More precisely, it's this:
I have boundaries, but my boundaries are easily moved by attraction, goodwill, desire, needs and exchanges of resources.
You know where the proper limit is.
It's just that when desire appears, attraction grows stronger and a relationship starts to flow, that line may begin to move.

So here is the difference:
facing each other, you use the Seeker's ways to get others to step inside the line Lian Zhen has drawn.
Sharing a palace, Lian Zhen's judgment of boundaries is itself blended with the Seeker's desire and attraction.

One is: "How do I get others to accept this line?"
The other is: "What moves my line itself?"

Has my interpretation helped you understand these two configurations better?
