If the Seeker (Tan Lang) is a "high-attraction personality," then the Sun (Tai Yang) is a high-public-responsibility personality.
The Seeker's pain is weak boundaries and being easily projected onto;
the Sun's pain is more hidden, and also crueler:
a gap between expectations and reality. It wants to be the core, but doesn't get the treatment of the core.

Both of these stars are easily misunderstood:
the Seeker gets condemned as "lewd and base,"
the Sun gets described as "a life of toil, harming one's father and wife."
But if you truly understand the Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu,
you'll discover one thing: the ancients weren't insulting anyone,
they were describing a personality structure that is very modern, and also very cruel.

First, get hold of the Sun's "skeleton"
The Sun involves a great many ideas. To help you grasp the key points in one go,
the following seven points are the "core structure of the Sun"
drawn from the e-book I'm currently writing.
And you'll find that
all seven points correspond directly to the logic of the original text of the Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu.

║ The Sun's seven core ideas
1｜The Sun's core quality is gui (esteem)
"Gui" means scarcity and irreplaceability

2｜The Sun is about "functional expectations," not "guarantees from reality"
The Sun =
the public position you want to stand in + the function you're willing to take on,
but there's no guarantee the world will repay you the way you imagine.

3｜The Sun's real pain isn't exhaustion, but the "gap between expectations and reality"
The gap between expectations and reality = in your heart you're the core, but in reality you're treated as a spare part filling in gaps.

4｜The most fatal blind spot: treating "being needed" as "self-worth"
"I'm needed = I matter"

Once the Sun lives out "gui" as "being needed,"
it will keep expanding the scope of its responsibilities until it hollows itself out.

5｜What function does the Sun serve in the system?
Just grasp three classical phrases:
the exemplar of creation / the pivot of office and rank / the circuit of the heavens
In plain words, that's three things:
a public benchmark × a key node × long-term, stable output

6｜The essence of the Sun with Hua Ji
You're used as the core, but not acknowledged as the core.
= The responsibility is yours, but the power is not.

7｜The behavioral trajectory after things fall out of balance
When even its original "self-burning method" can no longer hold up,
the Sun will fill bigger gaps with bigger output.
The common path of evolution is: structural adjustment → change of scene → rewriting of values → depletion and burnout

║ The Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu
On the surface, the Sun signifies "warmth, justice, loving to take care of people."
But what its deeper content really describes is
a role structure with high public responsibility, but very high risk.

The essence of the Sun isn't warmth; it's three things that must all hold at once:
public coordinates × authorized responsibility × sustained output

And that's why the Sun creates a psychological feeling in the whole field: a sense of order (stability).

You'll naturally be pushed into that position: "you must carry it, hold it up, make the call."

This is exactly why the verdicts on the Sun's fate are so extreme:
•	When it goes well → esteem, a path upward, being put to use by the system
•	When it goes astray → working your heart out and wearing yourself down, a title that doesn't match reality, and even conflict in relationships
The "toil, legal trouble, illness, harming one's father and relatives" in the classical text aren't curses;
they describe a structural consequence:

high responsibility × low resources × high visibility → the cost is magnified, and the pressure inevitably spills over.

║ A modern retranslation of key sentences from the Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu
① "The Sun belongs to Fire; it is the essence of the sun. It is the exemplar of creation."
The Sun isn't "heat"; it's the light source itself.
It's not emotion, nor ability,
but this: the moment you appear, you're seen.
"The exemplar of creation" means: the Sun is a symbol of order that is seen.
When you stand there, the whole field begins to align with you.

•	Social psychology: High Visibility Role
•	Systems science: Reference Point / Central Signal
You may not be the best at getting things done, but you let everyone know which way to go now (decided by you).

② "In the chart, it governs a person of noble bearing, able in both letters and arms."
The Sun's "gui" isn't about being well liked,
but this: you have the potential to be authorized and entrusted with important posts.
•	Letters: setting rules, defining direction
•	Arms: carrying out rulings, bearing the consequences
You're not just someone who does the work; you're the one who must decide, and also be responsible for the results.

③ "When the auspicious stars gather, it brings blessings; when it sits among dark stars, it toils in heart and strength."
With support, you shine; without support, you can only force yourself to hold on.
The question has never been whether you're shining,
but whether there are people, or a system, that can "catch" your light.
This corresponds exactly to the JD–R Model in workplace psychology:
high responsibility × low support → emotional exhaustion (burnout).

④ "It is the pivot of office and rank."
"Pivot," in plain words: without you, the process gets stuck.
The reason the Sun becomes "gui" isn't that it's likable,
but that the system needs you.
Name doesn't mean fame, but formal acknowledgment by the system: you can carry responsibility.

⑤ "The Sun travels the circuit of the heavens, turning without end."
This sentence doesn't describe a person's state, but the celestial nature of the Sun.
The sun moves along the ecliptic, cycle after cycle, year after year, never stopping.
It symbolizes a role of constant, sustained output that is not allowed to be interrupted.

When this celestial image of "perpetual output" is mapped onto a person,
it forms a very particular personality structure:
you're not expected to "shine occasionally," but expected
to always be present, always be available, always burn yourself to light up everyone.

What modern psychology finds in this
At the level of life and career, this leads to a common phenomenon:
•	you have almost no room to "switch off"
•	your role isn't allowed to disappear
•	once the structure falls out of balance, depletion keeps accumulating

So in life, Sun-type roles often follow a trajectory like this:
shining → taking on → accumulating depletion → being forced to adjust the structure
→ The point isn't "whether the Sun will burn,"
→ but whether there is a structure that can catch this "perpetual burning."

This star, the Sun,
has never been a matter of willpower,
but a matter of position.
Is the position you stand in
worthy of the light you're burning?

When the light no longer reaches any value,
the next star appears: the Star of Obscurity (Ju Men).
