There's a star in the old texts that's written up like a destroyer of lives.
Its person is violent, fierce and cunning; crafty by nature; rarely gets along with others; readily harms people;
never helps others do good, but is good at helping others do evil;
treats the six kinds of kin with cruelty, like bandits and enemies; deals with flesh and blood without kindness or righteousness.

And there are descriptions for each palace:
in the Palace of Self, in a weak position: abandons ancestors and leaves the clan
in the Palace of Siblings: flesh and blood estranged
in the Palace of Love and Partnership: improper
in the Palace of Children and Creation: first loss, then success
in the Palace of Money Management: like hot water poured on snow
in the Palace of the Outer World: runs about without strength
in the Palace of Daily Focus: brings poverty
in the Palace of Home and Property, in a weak position: the ancestral foundation squandered
in the Palace of Inner Life and Fortune: many disasters

After reading that, don't you feel this star is about as bad as it gets?
So here's the question: which star is written so miserably in the old texts?
The answer is Po Jun, the Star of Depletion.

Today, let's clear the Star of Depletion's name.

The Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu was written against the backdrop of traditional scholar-official society.
In that era, the most important value came down to one word: order.
Order between ruler and minister
Proper relations between father and son
Laws handed down by ancestors
Rules within the clan

In such a society, the most dangerous person wasn't a bad person,
but someone who didn't keep to the order.

And the Star of Depletion is exactly the star that does this.

Read objectively, many of the old texts' descriptions mean:
doesn't keep the ancestral legacy → they just don't keep to the existing system
rarely gets along with others → they just won't obey
readily harms people → disrupts existing interests
abandons ancestors and leaves the clan → breaks away from the traditional system

So in the eyes of ancient society, such a person was of course terrifying.

The old text says: "Po Jun belongs to Water and is the seventh star of the Northern Dipper.
In the heavens it is killing energy; in the chart it is the star of depletion; so its core quality is called hao (depletion)."
The key comes down to two words: killing energy, and depletion.

When many people see "hao," they think it means waste, squandering the family fortune.
But go back to the context of the old texts, and it doesn't.
What "hao" really stands for is the cost of changing a system.

Any force that breaks an existing order consumes resources.
Starting a business consumes resources
Revolution consumes resources
War consumes resources
Technological breakthroughs consume resources too

Because when you begin
breaking order,
overturning structures,
changing systems,
the whole system generates "dissipation."

So what the Star of Depletion really stands for in the old texts is the power to disrupt a system.
It isn't good, and it isn't evil.
What it stands for is change.

That's why the old texts made the Star of Depletion sound so frightening.
Because in ancient society, the most frightening person wasn't a bad person,
but someone who didn't keep to the order.

But in today's world, very often,
those who don't keep to the order are the ones who change the situation:
entrepreneurs
disruptors
tech innovators
breakers of systems

What they do is, in essence, very "Star of Depletion."
So is the Star of Depletion good or not?
The answer is actually simple: it depends on whether you want to preserve what exists, or to change it.

In a society that preserves what exists, the Star of Depletion is a troublemaker.
But in an era of change, the Star of Depletion may well be the one who opens a new game.

So the Star of Depletion isn't bad.
It's just the kind of person
who, once they make a move, makes the whole system "pay a price."

Think about it...
Trump
Musk
Many people find them obnoxious,
they don't play by the rules, and often even flip the table.

But sometimes,
flipping the table and starting over is itself a way to break a deadlock.

So, which palace is your Star of Depletion in?
That area is very likely where you are
willful,
reckless,
but at the same time full of
courage, passion, creativity and disruptive power.
