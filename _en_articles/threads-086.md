║ Zi Wei Dou Shu
The fourteen major stars have different ways of recovering.

The Keeper of the Seal (Tian Xiang) | After reason collapses, rebuilding grace and order
Pain point: when order is disrupted and its sense of fairness collapses, the Keeper of the Seal's grace is gone, and the fury and extremes of the Star of Depletion (Po Jun) opposite surge up: wanting to reason things out, and also wanting to wreck the whole situation in one go
First aid: don't rush to prove yourself or win them back. First get away from the scene and from the emotional scene, and get your body moving (walking, cleaning, sweating) to clear the chaos out of your head
Long term: the Keeper of the Seal can't stand disorder, so first bring the non-romantic parts of life back to a normal rhythm: regular meals, sleep, order at work.
Once reality is stable, replace emotional discussions with clear rules, redefine the relationship's boundaries and responsibilities, and let reason and dignity return to their places

The Guardian (Tian Liang) | Turning kindness back toward yourself
Pain point: still taking care of the other person after the breakup, heartbroken yet worried they won't be okay. The Guardian always wants to "save people," but often forgets it needs to be taken care of too
First aid: the Guardian is the star most likely to repay wrongs with kindness. First, stop making excuses for them and cleaning up after them. Carry out a "love yourself" list: eat, sleep, get some sun, take a walk. Only once you've done all four are you allowed to talk about your ex
Long term: volunteer and do charity, giving love to those who need it, and take some of your empathy back for yourself. Don't become your ex's emotional shelter; learn to give your kindness boundaries

The Resolute Star (Qi Sha) | Breaking out through action
Pain point: I'm hurting, but I'm not saying a word (swallowing the bitterness): calm on the outside, churning inside. The Resolute Star doesn't easily voice its pain, always feeling "I just need to get through it"

First aid: talking it out is an emotional outlet too. If you can't say it, write it down; if there's no one to tell, get moving. High-intensity exercise lets the body release painkillers, endorphins, an antidote for the heartache.
Long term: the Resolute Star, the best at enduring, should most beware of "forcing itself through," thinking that not talking and not thinking will make it heal. The truly strong are those who dare to face their wounds. In the process of sweating, creating and rebuilding, slowly become again the you who has direction and drive.

The Star of Depletion (Po Jun) | Rebooting with someone new is my therapy
Pain point: the Star of Depletion's love is all or nothing. Once it collapses, it has the instinct of "better to be a shattered jade than an intact tile": either be reborn, or go down together.
First aid: don't waste this energy on destruction. Turn the power of "breaking" into drive: creating, traveling, remaking your space all work.
Carry out "break first, then build" micro-actions: throw out three old things + start one new habit, so your feel for life reboots.
Long term: the key to the Star of Depletion's healing isn't forgetting, but "reshaping." Keep using action to create a new version of yourself, rewriting your life's script as "I'm in charge." Don't be afraid to start over; this time, even the wounds can become art.

Every star has its own rhythm of healing:
some put their lives back in order, some regain trust through kindness,
some break out through action, and some are reborn in the ruins.
Heartbreak isn't an ending, but the beginning of growing yourself back.
