In the last post we said mindfulness is "deliberately placing your attention on the present moment, and noticing your body, thoughts and emotions without judgment."

So how do you practice mindfulness? There are three core elements:
1\. Intentional attention
Don't let your mind wander; deliberately place your attention on one object.
→ The simplest way: focus on your breath, or quietly feel the sensations of your body and the sounds around you.

2\. A non-judging attitude
It isn't about forbidding yourself from having thoughts, but being able to notice: "Ah, I'm criticizing / worrying."
→ Notice first, then let go, without running after the emotion.

3\. Focusing on the present
Don't dwell on the past, and don't rush to worry about the future.
→ Practice feeling: the rise and fall of your breath, the emotions in your heart, the sounds in your ears.

These three core elements are like three steps for when "emotions strike": notice → accept → return to the present.
With long-term practice, you can gradually get free of being held hostage by insecurity, anger and anxiety.

Zi Wei Dou Shu ║ Stars with Hua Ji
In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the stars represent a person's abilities and personality, and Hua Ji is a change a star undergoes at a particular time and place.
When a star takes Hua Ji, a "gap" appears in its traits, bringing emptiness, problems, unease and frustration.

For example:
at work, you and Mr. Tan are rivals. Lately everything's been going his way: he landed a big deal, won the general manager's appreciation, even got a new car. You can't help feeling agitated: "Wasn't I the favorite before?"

So you start thinking about using your connections, even stepping into gray areas, to grab the next deal.

At this point, the Star of Restraint (Lian Zhen) in your chart may take "Hua Ji" because of changes in time and environment.

║ The Star of Restraint with Hua Ji
•	The Star of Restraint's core quality is "qiu" (confinement); it originally stands for self-restraint, self-management, keeping one's desires confined.
•	With Hua Ji, this restraint loses its balance, and it instead uses cleverness and connections to take shortcuts.
•	Though effective in the short term, without principles it can turn into opportunism, ultimately triggering a crisis of trust and norms.

║ How mindfulness helps
When the Star of Restraint's Hua Ji flares up, the heart is pushed along by "comparison and anxiety."
That's exactly when the three core elements of mindfulness come in handy:

•	Intentional attention: bring your attention back to your breath, stepping away from outside comparisons for a while.
•	A non-judging attitude: notice "I'm jealous, I'm anxious," without rushing to deny it or go along with it.
•	Focusing on the present: return to what you need to do right in front of you, rather than getting stuck in the loop of "he has it and I don't."

Hua Ji brings a gap, and mindfulness reminds us: don't rush to make up for it; first notice and accept, then decide how to respond.
