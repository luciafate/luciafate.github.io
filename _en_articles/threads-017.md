Fixing the chart:
if you know only the year, month and day of birth but not the hour, how do you work backward to the birth hour?
Everyone has their own habits; here's mine:
first pick out 4 to 5 clear life events that have already happened to the person.
Because for people born on the same day, several hours may indeed fit a given event; that's normal.
So I first list the possible hours for each event, then find where they overlap.

A made-up example, using the ten-year chart:
Event A: Zi, You, Hai
Event B: Yin, Shen, Hai
Event C: Chen, Xu, Hai
Event D: Hai, late Zi, Chou
Event E: Mao, Hai
And there's your answer.
So for the well-known figure in the bribery case from my previous post, if you look up his life events, you can pin it down.

After that post, some friends and Threads followers left a question I thought was a very good one, so I've put my answer together to share with everyone:
Could everyone born in the same year, month, day and hour take bribes?
Answer: no.

People born in the same year, month, day and hour, even with identical charts, won't have identical lives.
Because everyone grows up in a different environment, comes into contact with different people and things, has different experiences of interaction and different narratives, and ends up shaped with different values.

Sharing a chart at most means similar signs and similar behavioral tendencies are more likely to appear; it doesn't mean ending up with the same outcome.
That said, unless they have strong self-awareness, most people still tend to follow their own habits and the structure of their chart, so people who share a chart really do often show some similar patterns of behavior.

For example: suppose person A was born in the same year, month, day and hour as the doctor in that post. When A reaches the sixth ten-year cycle, A may also show a tendency at work to "use their position to take benefits they shouldn't."
But if A works as a cleaner at a company,
versus as a civil servant,
both charts show taking improper benefits through the authority of their job in violation of the rules, and both show signs of guan fei, but because their environments and responsibilities differ, the consequences differ (a civil servant would face criminal liability).

This touches on something very important:
people's choices really do matter.
You are never completely without choice.
(And this is actually changing your fortune in a broad sense.)

Here's a more extreme example:
suppose a person is, by nature, a thrill-seeker,
and their timing charts show future signs of bleeding and physical injury.

But whether they madly go off to the Russia–Ukraine war as a mercenary,
or madly take up extreme sports,
or choose to play paintball or go car racing,

all of these could fulfill the signs of bleeding and physical injury, BUT the environments differ, and so the consequences differ quite a lot.

The chart is a structure.
It reminds you where things are likely to go wrong,
but what environment you put yourself in, and how you react to things, are still yours to choose.

Of course, changing your fortune doesn't mean that once you foresee everything, you can definitely change it.
Very often, people can still only change the parts they can control, preventing and preparing in advance.

But "knowing in advance"
and being hit completely unprepared when something happens
are different in their psychological impact.
