You surely know someone like this: not outgoing, not a smooth talker, yet able to make friends wherever they go.
No need to envy them; after reading this, you can be just as good at it.

The Like Switch was written by Jack Schafer, a former FBI agent and instructor who was originally responsible for training intelligence officers to win the goodwill, even the cooperation, of people under conditions of extreme distrust. He distilled this set of "recruitment techniques" into a handbook ordinary people can use.
Friendship isn't a matter of fate; it's a psychological mechanism that can be worked.

Since this formula lets FBI agents build trust even with adversaries,
following it in daily life is like flipping this "like switch":
others will naturally like you and trust you.

1\. The friendship formula: do these 4 things, and people naturally come closer

Friendship = proximity + frequency + duration + intensity

Simply put:
see each other often, keep interactions light, be natural together, and let the other person know you notice them.

① Proximity: let them see you (not cling to them)
Not following them, but letting yourself "naturally be there":
• running into each other at the water cooler
• replying in the group chat now and then
• going to the gym at the same time
• smiling and nodding when you meet
The brain is very honest: the more often you see someone, the more familiar they feel.

② Frequency: short, light and frequent works better than deep talks

A 10-second greeting, a nod in the elevator, liking a post on Threads or IG, a smile in passing.
The golden combination in socializing is high frequency × low burden.

③ Duration: being able to stay together is getting closer
Not forcing conversation, but "naturally being there together":
walking together, attending a class, waiting in line, tidying up after an event.
Being able to be quietly in the same space together is proof of "trust."

④ Intensity: one line of "I noticed you…" is the final push
No need for deep talks.
One small observation can quickly build up feeling:
"You look a little tired today?"
"That part of your presentation just now was great."
"Have you been busier lately?"
Subconsciously, everyone wants to be noticed.

2\. Friend signals: make people like you without saying a word
Smiling, soft eye contact, nodding, a slight head tilt, a relaxed body, a quick eyebrow flash.
Humans judge whether you're safe within 0.1 seconds.
Your body language is your "business card of goodwill."

3\. Misattribution: show up when they're in a good mood, and they'll fall for you
This is psychology.
When the other person sees you while they're in a good mood,
the brain automatically links "happiness" with "you."
Situations it works in: after coffee, after exercise, after finishing work, at dinner gatherings, at celebrations, on a nice day.

4\. Holding back a little: not playing hard to get, but a sense of boundaries
The most comfortable way to socialize isn't "burning with enthusiasm," but:
• friendly but not people-pleasing
• willing to interact but not at their beck and call
• clear about your own rhythm
People with clear boundaries are the most relaxing to be around (the perfect way to play it cool).

5\. Self-disclosure: share just 10%, and you'll get trust in return
No need to tell secrets.
Just reveal a little of your daily life:
"I've been learning to cook lately; I've failed ten times."
"I've been running every day lately, my legs are dead."
"I'm reading a really great book."
You share a little, they share a little, and trust builds up just like that.

6\. The Like Switch SOP
If you don't know how to start making friends, follow these five steps:
1 | Show up often: don't be invisible
2 | Short, light and frequent: smile, nod, 10-second greetings
3 | Spend more time together: be naturally together in the same space
4 | One line of "I noticed you": the most effective emotional upgrade
5 | Friend signals + empathy + common ground: create a sense of safety

3\. The 14 major stars × The Like Switch
The stars of Zi Wei Dou Shu are, in essence, different personality traits.
If you want to get to know someone, an attractive guy or girl, a colleague, a friend of a friend, but you're not sure how to approach them, besides using the FBI's "friendship formula," you can also pair it with "the stars in Zi Wei Dou Shu" to raise your success rate a lot.

The Zi Wei Dou Shu method depends on how much information you have about the other person. There are two situations:

Situation one: the other person is someone you just pass on the street or nod to; they haven't even noticed you yet
→ Then look at the major stars in the Palace of the Outer World and Palace of Siblings (your circle of weak ties) of the monthly cycle in your own chart.
Because during this period, you're most likely to attract, meet or naturally get close to people with the qualities of these stars.

Situation two: the other person is in your work circle, a different department or a different social circle, but someone you have chances to meet (find a way to learn their Chinese zodiac sign)
→ You can convert their zodiac sign into an Earthly Branch position, then look at the major star in the "corresponding Earthly Branch palace" of your chart. (This technique is called Tai Sui ru gua.)
For example: they were born in the Year of the Dog → the Earthly Branch is Xu → look at which star sits in the Xu position of your own chart.

This shows the way you and the friend you want to make can interact.
Because the relationship between palaces is how the two of you treat each other, and they also want you to treat them this way.

Why does this method work?
Because in a particular time and setting, the stars in different palaces of your chart "attract" or "meet" people with similar qualities.
Refer to your own stars and pair them with the FBI formula, and you'll know
the most likely way you and the other person will interact, the strategy for getting closer, and the language of friendship that will succeed.

║ The major star in the palace:
1 | The Emperor (Zi Wei): respect me, and I'm willing to get close to you
The Emperor values "being valued," "being treated with courtesy" and "being held in esteem." The more measured, steady and appropriate you are, the more naturally the distance shrinks.
Key to friendship: keep your poise, show professionalism, make them feel you respect their position.
One line of "I noticed":
Breaking the ice with a complete stranger: "You look really well dressed today." (The Emperor instantly lights up on hearing "my taste has been noticed.")
Weak-tie connections (work settings, the same event, the same circle): "What you said just now was spot on; I really agree."
