Warren Buffett, CEO of Berkshire Hathaway, has released his Thanksgiving letter to shareholders.
In most people's eyes, this "farewell letter," possibly his last formal shareholder letter, is about succession, longevity and philanthropy.
But if you look closely, you'll find he spends most of it counting off his "friends from Omaha."
At first glance it's homely reminiscence: which street, which neighbor, who once lived a hundred yards across the road.
But if you fill in the titles behind those names,
a president of Coca-Cola, a newspaper leader, an energy company director, Berkshire's successor,
you'll understand:
Buffett is showing off the scarcest asset of his life: a network with a low cost of trust.

║ How do referrals from people you know, and private circles, grow?
These connections weren't gained through socializing; they were "cultivated":
• a shared community background (same town, same school, same industry) →
• long-term observation of character (frugal, keeps promises, down to earth) →
• small, repeated tests through working together →
• behavior patterns that are "predictable" to each other →
• in the end, driving the cost of trust extremely low

Only when the cost of trust is low enough to ignore do you really have room to spend your time creating value.

Another example is Silicon Valley's PayPal Mafia.
Members of this team later went on to found LinkedIn, YouTube and Tesla: arguably "the greatest startup team in history."

The reason is simple:
during their PayPal days they built a network of high mutual trust,
knowing one another's abilities, personalities and ways of working.
So later, when starting companies, finding partners or seeking investment, they all vouched for one another within the circle.

Omaha is Buffett's private network;
PayPal is a microcosm of entrepreneurs' private networks.

║ Say goodbye to high-cost socializing: the real shortcut is called "a trusted acquaintance"
The modern world is full of "high-cost socializing":
countless dinners, meetings, exchanges of business cards, tentative collaborations,
all to build a sliver of fragile trust.

The reality is:
apart from national examinations, life doesn't have that much open, fair competition.
Most of the really good opportunities don't appear in the public sphere; they circulate only in private circles.

If you know people in the circle, the opportunity is yours;
if you don't, you won't even know the opportunity ever existed.

The private sector cares about efficiency, not fairness.
• When a company urgently needs a certain skill, it doesn't wait for HR to advertise publicly; the CTO just calls out to their circle of acquaintances.
• When venture capitalists look for deals, it's usually through private connections, not open calls.

"Referrals from people you know" aren't a back door; they're the most reasonable risk control in a world of information asymmetry.
Because a candidate recommended by an insider is far safer than a stranger grabbed blindly from the open market.

The essence of a private circle isn't "a group of good friends who grew up together," but "weak ties":
friends of friends, partners you've worked with once, people who contributed in the same community.

There's only one key point:
you must be worthy of trust before you're invited into the private circle.

Let's look at how the "peach blossom stars" in your Zi Wei Dou Shu chart can open the door to connections for you.

║ The peach blossom stars in the chart: your inborn "water nature" for connections
In Zi Wei Dou Shu, the stars whose element is Water are the "peach blossom stars."

The qualities of water: it flows, it seeps through, it can be soft or strong. This is exactly the basic ability to connect with others and build a network.

The peach blossom stars can be roughly divided into:
• Yang Water: leaning toward social peach blossom (the Blessing Star (Tian Tong), the Keeper of the Seal (Tian Xiang))
easy to get along with, easy to work with, readily gaining support
• Yin Water: leaning toward romantic peach blossom (the Moon (Tai Yin), the Star of Depletion (Po Jun), the Star of Obscurity (Ju Men), the Seeker (Tan Lang))
attractive, very charming, often with a strong personal style

What are you best at in a private circle? Look at the stars in your Palace of Self, Palace of Daily Focus and Palace of Friends and Associates.

║ The Keeper of the Seal: network manager, neighborhood chief, professional endorser
Its core quality is "yin" (the Seal), standing for stamping, approval, trust.
People with the Keeper of the Seal:
• are good at arranging resources and matching needs
• know who should connect with whom, and who should be avoided
• value etiquette and order; they're the "endorsers" of the circle
The Keeper of the Seal is very often the person in a private circle everyone talks about: "Ask them, they'll know best who to go to."

║ The Blessing Star: the warm-up king of the public sphere
Its core quality is "fu" (blessing): optimistic, kind, not petty.
The Blessing Star is very well suited, in communities and the public sphere, to:
• sharing, writing posts, podcasts, organizing events
• keeping the atmosphere relaxed
• making people come up and ask questions on their own
The Blessing Star is the classic case of "contributing in the public sphere → automatically being pulled into private circles."

║ The Moon: the meticulous keeper of connections
Its core quality is "fu" (abundance); "abundance" includes not just money but also richness in emotional security.
The Moon's traits:
• gentle, meticulous, considerate
• soothing unease, reconciling conflicts
• doing private follow-up after social events
The Moon isn't the most active, but it's often the circle's "first choice for long-term collaboration."

║ The Star of Depletion: an expert at cold outreach, daring to charge in and take the field
Its core quality is "hao" (depletion): tearing down the old and building the new.
The Star of Depletion's traits:
• bold, open-handed, stylish, creative
• takes the initiative, not afraid of rejection, not afraid of awkwardness
• good at opening the first door, then handing things over to others to maintain
In a private circle, the Star of Depletion is the one who "creates opportunities."

║ The Seeker: the great peach blossom star, a node where connections flow
Its core quality is "peach blossom": desire, the power to get things moving.
The Seeker's traits:
• socially adept, polished on every side
• likes to play at life, likes experiences
• widely informed, able to move across circles
• many people asking around for news go straight to them
The Seeker is the intersection of "information flow + network flow."

║ The Star of Restraint (Lian Zhen): self-managed, good-looking peach blossom, with fatal attraction
The Star of Restraint's core quality is "qiu" (confinement), standing for self-restraint and self-management.
Its element, Yin Fire, isn't a peach blossom star, but its "peach blossom constitution" is extremely strong.
People with the Star of Restraint:
• are clever, flexible, quick to react
• are very likely to be good-looking
• are witty, charming, good at managing their image
If it shares a palace with peach blossom, such as
Lian Zhen–Tan Lang, Lian Zhen–Po Jun, Lian Zhen–Tian Xiang, or with Wen Qu,
its charm goes straight through the ceiling, to the level of "fatal attraction."

In a private circle it easily becomes:
• the person who's remembered the moment they speak
• the circle's barometer of trends
• a key node bridging across circles

║ How do you use your peach blossom stars to open up private circles?
Route one: contribute in the public sphere first, so insiders "see you."
The easiest, with the lowest barrier.
The public sphere needs no ticket:
writing, sharing, making content, small tests, professional contributions.
As long as you keep contributing, people in your field will "pull you into the private circle."
This method suits best:
• the Blessing Star → the warm-up king
• the Keeper of the Seal → serve first, then reap
• the Seeker → if it finds something fun, it dives in first

Route two: seize "temporary needs," trading problem-solving for trust.
When you hear or sense that a company is stuck, step in and solve it, and you have a chance to win trust (or leave an impression).
Especially suited to:
• the Star of Obscurity → good at taking problems apart
• the Star of Depletion → creative, bold, ready to open things up
• the Moon → doing long-term follow-up after solving it
Offline events and closed-door sharing make it easier for others to remember you.

Route three: build bridges between networks.
If you know people on both sides, you're the bridge.
Especially suited to:
• the Seeker → a born friend-maker
• the Star of Restraint → building personal charm through self-management
• the Keeper of the Seal → the circle's recognized "trustworthy endorser"
When you build bridges for others, you become an irreplaceable node in the private circle.

║ Private circles have always been there; they just won't come knocking on your door
Zi Wei Dou Shu shows us a possible direction:
where are your inborn networking abilities?
In what position are you suited to letting your peach blossom traits shine?

Is it the Blessing Star's goodwill?
The Keeper of the Seal's matchmaking?
The Moon's attention to detail?
The Star of Depletion's creativity and style?
The Seeker's broad knowledge and flexibility?
The Star of Restraint's charm and ability?
The Star of Obscurity's insight?

Private circles are there all along, but they won't come looking for you.
You have to first:
• contribute in the public sphere
• seize temporary needs
• build bridges for others

When you do this, you're not making friends.
You're building yourself "an Omaha of your own":

a place where trust can compound,
where opportunities pass between people who know each other,
and where your peach blossom stars can grow into a true "network asset."
