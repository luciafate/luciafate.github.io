When many people first read the Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu, they're easily misled by one line in it:
"What does the Hua Ji star govern?"

Since the book says
"What does the Hua Lu star govern?"
"What does the Hua Quan star govern?"
"What does the Hua Ke star govern?"
"What does the Hua Ji star govern?"
then why don't we treat Hua Lu, Hua Quan, Hua Ke and Hua Ji
as four stars?

The answer is actually simple: they aren't.
Because the way the old texts describe things is different from the logic of modern writing.

1. In the old texts, "star" is often just a general term
In the Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu, "star" sometimes doesn't refer to a specific star at all.
Often it's more of a functional label, used to describe a certain effect or symbol.
For example, expressions common in the book: the blessing star, the noble star, the benevolent star, the official star.

In some cases these may correspond to particular stars, but very often they're describing a symbol or an effect.

So something like "the Hua Lu star"
actually comes closer to meaning
the effect of lu,
the image of lu.

2. The Four Transformations are changes in the stars
The Four Transformations are changes that stars undergo because of "stimuli from the setting of time and place."
For example:
the Sun (Tai Yang) with Hua Lu
the Strategist (Tian Ji) with Hua Quan
Wen Chang with Hua Ke
the Star of Restraint (Lian Zhen) with Hua Ji

If Hua Lu, Hua Quan, Hua Ke and Hua Ji were each a star, there would be a problem:
each would need a fixed position.
But you've never seen a "Hua Lu star" appear in a palace, have you!

3. The fourteen major stars are roles; the Four Transformations are motives and behavior
Understood from a modern systems perspective, it becomes clearer:
the fourteen major stars
are "personality roles"

the Four Transformations
are "triggered motives and patterns of behavior"

Because life isn't only about "who you are," but also "how you'll act in a given situation (= stimuli from the outside setting of time and place)."

For example:
the Sun (role)
with Hua Lu → motivated by gui (esteem), increasing resources and opportunities
the Strategist (role)
with Hua Quan → motivated by shan (seeking better ways), taking control of the situation and decisions
the Star of Restraint (role)
with Hua Ji → because of qiu (confinement), boundaries turn into dilemmas and conflict

So what we see isn't where the Hua Lu star is,
but which star is operating in the manner of "lu."

The Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu also hints at this:
Lu is the spirit of fu de
the Quan star is the spirit that holds judgment over life and death
the Ke star governs letters and writing
Ji is the spirit that meddles in many things

This is actually describing four kinds of effect.
Lu
increases resources

Quan
takes control and decides

Ke
is seen and affirmed

Ji
obstruction, a gap

In short:
the fourteen major stars are a person's character.
The Four Transformations are how that character behaves "in this setting of time and place."

Understand Zi Wei Dou Shu this way,
and the whole system suddenly becomes very clear.
Many people think
Zi Wei Dou Shu is fortune-telling.
It's actually
a system that describes "personality × patterns of behavior."

So please, stop treating Hua Ji as a star.
If Master Xiyi found out... he'd want to strangle you!
