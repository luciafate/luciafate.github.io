Your investments keep losing money, and you know you should cut your losses, yet you hang on because you can't bear to accept it?

A relationship has become nothing but torment, yet you never quite find the courage to leave?

In life there are always things you know you should cut off, stop or walk away from, but you just can't bring yourself to do it.

Today I'll show you a method. You don't need to buy lucky charms, and you don't need to master advanced chart-reading techniques first.

Because there is already a blade in your chart; you just may never have noticed it.

## There is already a blade in your chart

Open your natal chart and find which palace Qing Yang falls in.

Qing Yang is one of the Four Sha Stars. We can picture it as a large blade: it stands for decisiveness, persistence, the nerve to act, and less avoidance of direct confrontation.

When the Complete Book of Zi Wei Dou Shu discusses Qing Yang in a strong placement (ru miao), it describes it as "firm and resolute in nature, resourceful in strategy and fond of daring."

In plain words: strong in temperament, able to decide once convinced; able to work out a method, and more willing to face risk.

So Qing Yang is a force that, once a judgment has formed, quickly gathers the will, breaks through obstacles, makes the decision and puts it into action.

In other words:

**Once convinced, blade in hand, move forward.**

The palace where your natal Qing Yang sits can help you see in which area of life you more readily show decisiveness, persistence and a willingness to face confrontation.

## In 2026 and 2027, everyone carries a blade

Besides Qing Yang in the natal chart, there is also Qing Yang in the timing cycles!

In 2026 and 2027, everyone's annual Qing Yang falls in the same position as their annual Palace of Self.

In 2026 that is the Wu position among the Earthly Branches; in 2027, the Wei position.

In these two years, prompted by the outside environment, everyone carries something like a blade.

But because each person's annual Palace of Self overlaps a different natal palace and ten-year palace, what each person really needs to deal with will differ.

## What can this blade be used for?

The very things I mentioned at the start: investments that need another look, relationships that need clarifying or cutting off, and effort that's going nowhere and should stop.

You can re-examine your boundaries and decide whether something should continue, be adjusted, or end.

How do you put it to use in practice?

The palaces of the monthly and daily cycles rotate over time.

If you want to deal with money, look for the period when the timing-cycle Palace of Money Management overlaps the position where Qing Yang sits; for relationships, pick the period when the timing-cycle Palace of Love and Partnership overlaps Qing Yang.

When that period comes, you can make the related issues your top priority to examine and deal with.

## Before you act, ask yourself three questions

Before using Qing Yang's force to bring change to your life, ask yourself first:

**What exactly am I cutting off? Can I bear the consequences?**

**Do I have enough information?**

**At what point should I stop?**

Many people fear the Sha stars and assume they must be bad.

But look at it another way: sometimes the reason life stays stuck in one place is precisely the lack of a blade willing to make a decision.

Kindness is precious, but kindness needs boundaries too.

So making good use of Qing Yang's force means letting decisiveness guard the boundaries of your kindness:

once you've thought it through and weighed the consequences, take a stand when it's time, cut your losses when it's time, and have the courage to leave when it's time.

**Before you act, see clearly; after you act, take responsibility, let go, and keep moving forward.**

## When should you sheathe the blade instead?

One more reminder.

When the structures related to Qing Yang in the timing charts carry too many Sha stars and Hua Ji, or they are overly concentrated, Qing Yang is also more likely to come under strain, turning into judging too early, forceful collisions, fighting over things, causing harm, or leaving no room at all to turn back.

At times like these, sheathe the blade first:

slow down your decisions, fill in the missing information, listen to different opinions, and leave yourself room to adjust.

Because truly mature decisiveness isn't rushing to act. It's knowing when to act, how far to go, and when to stop.

All right, go and find out: which palace does your natal Qing Yang fall in?

Then ask yourself:

**In 2026 and 2027, what is the one thing I most need to deal with decisively?**
