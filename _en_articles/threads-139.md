The last post introduced Wang Xifeng, the great housekeeper of Dream of the Red Chamber. Today I'll try to reconstruct her Palace of Self "small team": the star combinations of her Palace of Self, Outer World, Money Management and Daily Focus.

In Zi Wei Dou Shu you can't read just one palace. The Palace of Self's small team (the Palace of Self's san fang si zheng) can piece together a person's way of doing things and life focus more precisely.

[Palace of Self: the Keeper of the Seal (Tian Xiang)]
Born with a trustworthy friendliness and a power to bring people together; knows the rules, and knows flexibility too. On the surface she's mild and smooth, but her bottom line is clear (a bottom line she decides herself): the kind of "smooth leader" with a needle hidden in her smile. Applied to Wang Xifeng, you'll see that in front of people she's polished on every side and silver-tongued, but in fact every step is precisely calculated, and when it's time to be tough, she doesn't go easy. Her leadership style isn't head-on confrontation, but firmness within softness, knowing how to use relationships and rules to reach her goals.

[Palace of the Outer World: Lian Zhen–Po Jun (the Star of Restraint with the Star of Depletion) in the same palace]
This combination on the Self–Outer World axis makes her especially bold and skillful in dealing with the outside world.
The daring of the Star of Depletion and the shrewdness of the Star of Restraint make her able to see through rules quickly, operate flexibly, and even act against the rules. At work, a person like this makes their move without hesitation at the critical moment, and is good at working gray areas, able both to flatter and to turn hostile, leaving people both respecting and fearing them.
This kind of person will "pick your pocket with a smile," keeping up appearances in public while not holding back behind the scenes. She uses the system to cover herself and relationships to expand her power: a master at playing politics.
So when Sister Feng's temper flares, she's high-handed and rough, ruthless with the servants, and things like diverting household funds to earn high interest for her own pocket come as no surprise.

[Palace of Inner Life and Fortune: Zi Wei–Qi Sha (the Emperor with the Resolute Star) in the same palace]
Inwardly extremely proud and firm, with a clear sense of mission and ambition. The emperor star in the Palace of Inner Life and Fortune gives off a "royal" air from the bones out: proud inside, concerned with vision and scale, with a sense of mission and responsibility. Under pressure this combination is even more resolute; it doesn't fear challenges, and meeting resistance only makes it fight harder, with a ruthless edge.

[Palace of Money Management: the Chancellor (Tian Fu)]
An extremely strong ability to control money, resources and connections. She understands investment and risk management, and knows well the logic of hiding wealth "in herself" and of "controlling resources through the system." Wang Xifeng's ability to control the Jia household's accounts office is a display of exactly this.

When you meet Tian Xiang, look at Tian Fu; when you meet Tian Fu, look at Tian Xiang: the palaces where these two stars sit must be considered together. Don't think that just because the Tian Fu axis is in good shape, you can plan and control everything; you also need to look at the state of the Tian Xiang axis. Think about it: when you want to do something bad (break the rules) and don't want to get caught, won't you call on Tian Fu's deep scheming + planning and control? (If there are lots of Sha stars and Hua Ji, better not do it.)

[Palace of Daily Focus: Wu Qu–Tan Lang (the Treasurer with the Seeker) in the same palace]
This combination represents someone who works hard but is flexible and adaptable. The Treasurer represents discipline, responsibility and execution, while the Seeker brings charm and variety. Wang Xifeng's "doing what she says" and "ordering today, executing tomorrow" fit this structure exactly, letting her move with ease in a complex workplace, and even become the boss's most relied-upon right hand.

I chose this chart because it shows Wang Xifeng's skill, scheming and organizational ability, and also echoes the two extremes of her fate: from limitless glory at the height of the Jia household's power, to the tragic ending of the family's decline, the confiscation of its property, and her being cast off as a wife.
This chart has a great many empty palaces, symbolizing big variables and high flexibility in life; if fortune falls out of balance, defeat comes like a landslide. But all of this is also the karmic result driven by her own nature, made by her own hand.

Master the logic of reading the Palace of Self's "san fang si zheng," and you'll be able to quickly see through a person's core personality, way of doing things and potential risks. So stop reading one palace and one star in isolation.

No palace fights alone; each has its own "small team." Once you know how to observe the chart through this three-dimensional framework, you're closer than most people to the true, whole-chart wisdom of Zi Wei Dou Shu.
