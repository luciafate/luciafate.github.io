Do you have AI anxiety?
I do.
The first time I realized that AI's comprehension, reading, synthesis and speed of creation,
even its ability to "get the main point,"
were steadier and clearer than mine, and "less anxious" than me,
honestly, that moment wasn't panic,
but a very calm, very real thought surfacing:
I really could be replaced.

Leave aside whether "AI will rule humanity,"
no need to drag out the science-fiction scenario;
AI's current abilities alone
are already enough to keep many people from sleeping well.

Three kinds of people feel it most:
those who've spent years in the workplace, those who run their own businesses,
and those with children at home.

Because deep down we all sense one thing:
this time, the impact of AI may well go deeper than the Industrial Revolution.

║ So how did we "not feel anxious" before?
Simple.

We used to believe a logic as plain as it gets:
as long as you work hard enough, study well enough and get into a good school,
life will automatically unlock the next level.

The core motive for choosing a field of study was simple too:
learn a body of scarce knowledge and build a competitive moat.

That moat came from two things:
information asymmetry, and the cost of learning.

If you knew what others didn't,
or spent four years mastering a complex body of theory
that others couldn't learn,
then you had an advantage in the workplace.

In that era,
looking for a job was, in essence, one thing:
turning knowledge into money.

║ Since AI arrived, that logic has stopped working
What the AI era has truly overturned
isn't any one industry,
but something more fundamental:
knowledge has begun to be equalized.

Today, an ordinary person who knows how to write a prompt
can get, within seconds:
•	the full context of a historical question
•	a professional-looking draft legal agreement
•	code that runs
•	a tightly reasoned, well-structured analytical report

The "knowledge threshold" you spent years painstakingly building
is often crossed in less than a second
in front of AI.

This brings a very cruel reality that must be faced:
knowledge itself is no longer scarce.

║ What do humans have left?
The good news is:
what AI replaces is the threshold for acquiring knowledge,
but it can't replace something more crucial:
taking responsibility for outcomes.

The world still runs on exchange.
It's just that the core of the exchange
long ago stopped being how many skills you have,
and became whether you are willing, and able,
to bear a certain kind of consequence.

In any society,
the reason a person is needed and can obtain resources
has never been "what they can do,"
but that there are some things
they're willing to carry, and can carry.

This is exactly why I want to bring Zi Wei Dou Shu into this:
not for fortune-telling,
but to use it as
an analytical tool for understanding how people take on responsibility.

Stars falling in different palaces
describe the ways a person naturally reacts and takes on responsibility
when facing pressure, conflict and choices
in different situations.

What it truly helps you clarify
isn't "which line of work suits you,"
but
what kind of position of responsibility you're better suited to be in,
and whether that position
is compatible with your innate personality.
(Of course, some major stars span more than one role to begin with.)

Because every position that can last in a society
is, in essence, a kind of exchange:
you bear part of the risk, pressure or consequences for the system,
and society gives you back money, status, trust,
or at least
a position that can't easily be replaced.

║ Four "types of responsibility-bearing" in the AI era
1\. The interpretive type | bearers of meaning
What they bear: the consequences of recognized standing, legitimacy and order
These fields include:
literature, history and philosophy; law; management consulting; some business disciplines; institutional design.

What is AI good at?
Organizing material, writing first drafts, finding precedents, compiling viewpoints.
But that final step, AI can't provide:
defining meaning, balancing conflicts, putting its name behind an interpretation.

AI can write a contract,
but in a conflict of interests, "whom to protect, how, and why"
must be someone's responsibility.

AI can screen résumés,
but "whether this person suits this organization"
can't be decided by keyword matching.
A seasoned HR professional can often pick up, in a single interview,
the unquantifiable yet crucial, unspoken signals.

People suited to this type
tend to be sensitive to others and clear in logic,
good at using language and perspective to build order out of chaos.

Corresponding major stars:
•  The Keeper of the Seal (Tian Xiang; yin, the Seal): at its core, the Keeper of the Seal is about "maintaining institutions, appearances and order." It's good at being diplomatic within the rules, keeping conflicts from getting out of control and giving everyone a way to save face.

•  The Star of Obscurity (Ju Men; an, obscurity): Ju Men faces a world of "opaque information." It instinctively asks, takes things apart, clarifies gray areas, and uses language to turn the vague into a version that can be discussed.

•  The Star of Restraint (Lian Zhen; qiu, confinement): Lian Zhen deals with "boundaries and costs." It's sensitive to legal versus illegal, appropriate versus inappropriate; in gray areas, someone is most needed to step up and define the norms.

•  The Emperor (Zi Wei; zun, dignity): the Emperor is the star of "recognized standing and position." It isn't arguing about right and wrong, but guarding order and dignity: who has the final say, who must take responsibility, whether this account can hold up.

2\. The evidential type | bearers of truth
What they bear: the consequences of whether something is verified
These fields include:
the sciences, basic medicine, the social sciences, data analysis.

AI can run models, compute correlations and make predictions,
but it can't say for anyone:
"Having evaluated this conclusion, I dare to use it in the real world."
What a scientist is truly responsible for isn't the inference itself,
but whether that inference
can withstand testing.

What a doctor must bear is
judging what is most reasonable for this specific patient, and what treatment suits them best right now.

People suited to this type
usually have a fastidiousness about logic, data and cause and effect,
and are willing to spend time verifying rather than just wanting an answer.

Corresponding major stars:
•  The Strategist (Tian Ji; shan, seeking better ways): the Strategist's strength is "reasoning and correcting." Through constant hypothesis, revision and searching for better explanations, it pulls the world back from chaos into understandable, rational cause and effect.

•  The Guardian (Tian Liang; yin, shelter): the Guardian is like a "gatekeeper." It cares about experience, convention, legitimacy and long-term consequences, so it's especially suited to setting criteria: what can be used, and what shouldn't be.

•  The Chancellor (Tian Fu; quan, control): the Chancellor's "quan" isn't about throwing its weight around; it's about "holding resources and risks." It's good at turning evidence into decisions that can be put into practice, and bearing the responsibility those decisions bring.

3\. The constructive type | bearers of systems
What they bear: who is responsible when the system fails
These fields include:
software engineering, civil engineering, mechanical engineering, architecture, systems operations.

AI can draw plans, write code and propose solutions,
but it won't guarantee for you
that this system will keep working properly in the real, physical world,
even in extreme conditions.

What's more, the minimum standard of engineering isn't just "getting it built."
What the real world truly demands is
delivering, within the limits of budget, schedule and risk,
a system that runs for the long term and doesn't easily fail.

People suited to this type
usually have a high tolerance for efficiency, structure and consequences,
and are willing to bear responsibility for irreversible mistakes.

Corresponding major stars:
•	The Resolute Star (Qi Sha; sha, decisiveness): the Resolute Star is the executor in high-pressure environments. When time, risk and cost close in, it can make the call and carry the pressure of "if something goes wrong, it's on me."

•	The Treasurer (Wu Qu; cai, wealth): at its core, the Treasurer is about "effectiveness and control." It breaks a system down into costs, processes and metrics, seeking something that runs, can be calculated and can be maintained; what it fears most is spinning wheels and waste.

•	The Sun (Tai Yang; gui, esteem): the Sun is the star that "stands in front of everyone and lights the way." It's willing to take responsibility on itself, coordinating people and processes so the system can be trusted by many and keep running.

4\. The communicative type | bearers of emotion
What they bear: whether empathy, resonance and trust are established
These fields include:
media, advertising, art and design, psychological counseling, marketing.

AI can produce content in huge volumes,
but it can't give a brand a soul,
nor take in other people's emotions for anyone.

The value of this type lies in this:
can your work be trusted?
Can it be felt?
Can it last in an era when attention moves so fast?

This in itself is a highly draining kind of responsibility.

Corresponding major stars:
•  The Star of Depletion (Po Jun; hao, depletion): the Star of Depletion is the star of "breaking, reorganizing, replacing the old with the new." Creation and communication are draining to begin with, yet the Star of Depletion can open new ground amid the depletion and create differentiation.

•  The Moon (Tai Yin; fu, abundance): the Moon's abundance isn't showing off; it's "inner sensitivity and a sense of security." It can read emotions, create atmosphere and build trust, so people want to stay for the long term.

• The Seeker (Tan Lang; peach blossom): the Seeker is "attraction and connection" itself. It understands people, trends and interaction, turning content into relationships, relationships into spread, and single points into systems.

•  The Blessing Star (Tian Tong; fu, blessing): the Blessing Star is good at offering "a safe harbor of understanding and emotion." Its value isn't the amount of information, but letting people relax and feel held, so it's well suited to companionship, healing and giving a brand warmth.

So the real answer to AI anxiety
isn't learning more tools,
but
what responsibility you are willing to bear,
and whether this society is willing to pay a price for it.
